# The Docket, Read Aloud

## How to listen to this

This is the Speaker's docket for the First Session of the Mobilization Parliament. It is retold in plain speech for the President.

Here is what happened. Twenty-three councils sat for three rounds. They filed one hundred and twenty bills. They spent two hundred and thirty votes. Each council could spend at most three votes on one bill. No council could vote for its own bill. The Speaker merged the agreed ideas into seventeen coalition bills. Those bills now go to the President to sign.

Each unit, building and enemy is explained the first time it comes up, and marked as in the game today or only proposed.

## The Speaker's rulings

The Chair settled eleven points of order before the bills were counted.

First ruling. A bill called Pacing Hot said more industry should bring the fleet sooner, by shortening your preparation time. Its sponsor withdrew it. It broke Article Three, the rule that you may never be taxed for building. Its dread lives on in the second bill below. Industry will make the fleet arrive bigger. It will never make the fleet arrive sooner. The vote was unanimous.

Second ruling. Past two hundred and twenty terrain edits, the game silently undoes the oldest one. The Chair struck this as unconstitutional, because the player's work vanishes without a word. The fix is to bake old edits into the base ground instead. This is a quick fix.

Third ruling. The game quietly limits dangerous research. A cost is fine. A hidden cost is not. The limit becomes a visible choice, through the Charter bill.

Fourth ruling. The game treats a resource deposit as owned or not owned. So a second deposit of the same type does nothing. That is a bug. The count should matter. This is folded into the Deep Deposits bill and listed as a quick fix.

Fifth ruling. Psionic power will not get its own dedicated pool, because a pool is a cap. It is limited by slow, Relay-limited awakening and a stability cost instead. A Psionic Relay is a building in the game today. It projects fire rate and range, but lowers your stability cap.

Sixth ruling. Shapeshifters were meant to be immortal. They are a unit in the game today, a living nanoswarm with two hundred sixty health. Instead of immortality, the Chair chose heat. Their self-repair switches off above sixty heat, and above ninety they slow by thirty percent. Psi also wanted a heavy damage slump. The Chair struck it as a nerf. This one is flagged for the President.

Seventh ruling. A power shortage was going to cut fire rate. The sponsor withdrew it. A shortage is billed as industrial debt. It is never a throttle.

Eighth ruling. A rule gave a repeated propaganda campaign half effect. That is a diminishing return on a player tool, so it was struck. The fleet doubts after three identical lies instead.

Ninth ruling. A rule had lattice nodes absorb Mind-Forge damage. Struck. The nodes become killable cover.

Tenth ruling. The Stability council wanted an information policy dial. The President has said no sliders for market or command. The Chair ruled these are different things. The dial sets how you handle war information. It does not read your society. It is allowed. It is flagged for the President.

Eleventh ruling. The Privatise the Sky bill would have taxed commercial orbit. It had little support. Its debris-tracking structure lives on inside the Dead Sky Protocol. The commercial idea is not carried.

## What the councils found in the code

Three facts matter most. The fleet's adaptation scan has no air or sky-navy axis, so it never countered five straight air wins. Enemy strength scales with time only, so your industry does not frighten it. And Fire Control is dead code, because its switch is never set.

## The coalition bills

Votes are the second-round ballot totals of the bills merged into each coalition.

### The Thermal Field. Fifteen votes, the top of the ballot.

Sponsors were the energy, physics and psi councils. Railgun, game feel and science joined.

What it does. Every enemy gets one heat value. Lasers and plasma add heat. Plasma dumps a burst of heat on impact. Hot targets take up to fifty percent more kinetic damage. They also lose up to half their armor. So lasers cook, and guns finish.

Cooling depends on the medium. Air cools fast. Vacuum cools slowly, so targets in space stay hot. Re-entering pods arrive white-hot, which rewards kinetic weapons on the drop. Shapeshifters use the same heat field.

The Railgun Lance is a ground gun in the game today, with a range of a thousand. Tungsten Rods Zeus is an orbital unit in the game today. It drops kinetic spears from orbit.

Why. The councils wanted every weapon family to have a reason to exist beside the others. This is a combined arms bill. It is also the President's own seed.

Size. Large. New research called thermal load, which needs Directed Energy.

The Speaker recommends signing first.

### The Nerve. Twenty-three votes, the most of any bill.

Sponsors were autofleet, lore, pacing, stability and megaprojects. Psi joined.

What it does. The fleet gets a mind with two needles on one panel. Fear rises with your industry and output. It scales the fleet up, with more bombarders and more supers. The old ceiling of nine bombarders becomes soft. Fear never shortens the clock. Resolve falls with the fleet's losses. It drives four behaviour bands. These are Steady, Rethink, Despair and Calculated Retreat. In Rethink, the fleet picks a Decisive Strike or a planet-cracker flagship.

The panel shows what the fleet believes. The deception from the next bill can spoof it. A small tick shows the truth.

There are four ways to end the war. Attrition is today's withdrawal. Rout means killing the flagship. Orbital Denial means winning space superiority, and it needs Orbital Command and Space Age. That gives the space fleet a win condition at last. Truce means we stop if you stop. It is offered only at Despair or in the final cycle. A rethink cannot block an earned win. Its gate expires after ninety seconds.

The Autodrone Bombarder is an enemy superweapon in the game today. It is anchored in space.

Why. The old notes said the invasion plan was never explained. This makes the fleet a character with a mind.

Size. Large.

The Speaker recommends signing second, together with the next bill.

### The Fleet Reads You. Thirteen votes.

Sponsors were autofleet, air, naval, command, stability and psi.

What it does. The fleet's scan gets new axes. They are air, sky navy, command layer and psi. Each gets an answer.

The Reaver is a proposed enemy. It is an air-superiority hunter. It was proposed by the air council. It spawns only once you field twenty or more fighters.

The Weasel is a proposed enemy. It is an anti-radiation flight that homes on radars. The air council proposed it too.

The Durandal is a proposed enemy bomber that cracks runways. The air council proposed it. It only hits bases with eight or more parked aircraft.

Command-layer waves, called Cutting the Lines, go after your communications. They come from the command council. The Hush-Hound is a proposed light, fast enemy from the psi council. It carries a field that halves psi fire rate and range. It appears against psi mass.

The player side ships in the same bill. Hardened Shelters are proposed buildings that protect parked aircraft. Rapid runway repair fixes craters fast. EMCON drops the Weasel's lock. A Staff Bunker, proposed by the command council, keeps government running in a capitulation crisis.

Maskirovka means propaganda campaigns feed the fleet false intel. After three identical lies, the fleet starts doubting.

Why. Five wins with one doctrine is the clearest problem in the reports. This fixes it with a smarter enemy, not a weaker air force.

Size. Large.

The Speaker recommends signing second.

### The One Forecast. Eight votes.

Sponsors were new-player, pacing, market, science, lore, psi and stability.

What it does. One forecast model shows fleet size, mix, plan and arrival time as bands. The band width is forty percent, divided by the square root of one plus your observers. It never narrows below five percent. Observers are Radar, Data Center, sonar arrays, a Tracking Array and a new signals node. The exact arrival time is known from sixty seconds out.

Radar and the Data Center are buildings in the game today. The Tracking Array is a proposed building from the pacing council. It shows the return arc and the arrival time within five seconds.

Preparation plays as beats. They are deep contact, burn, manifest, split and re-entry. Warning lines at the first tier can be wrong. The chance is a quarter times one minus confidence. Two optional layers exist. One is the market's influence-staked positions. The other is the psi council's Listening Chamber. The Listening Chamber is proposed. It is an active ninety-second peek that costs stability cap, and the fleet hears it.

Why. This is the President's planned slice called forecast-sharpening intel buildings. It closes the oldest gap, that the invasion plan was never explained.

Size. Medium.

The Speaker recommends signing third.

### The Standing Plan. Eleven votes.

Sponsors were command, industry, market and new-player. Air, bio and missiles joined.

What it does. A Planning Bureau holds ledger lines. It is a proposed building from the command council. Each line says keep so many of a thing fielded, or build this here, or stock so many warheads. The plan auto-queues, auto-places and refills losses, spending your surplus. Command lines have fixed targets and a six percent planner's tax. Exchange lines let the market buy last fight's best kills per industrial capacity. Every blocked line shows what it needs, with a button to build the fix.

Why. This is the President's planned slice called surplus industrial outflow by what you built. It ends the two hundred twelve thousand industrial capacity hoard.

Size. Large.

Open for the President. Industry wants four industrial capacity per second per line. Command wants the six percent tax. The Chair recommends the tax.

The Speaker recommends signing third.

### Dead Sky Protocol. Fifteen votes.

Sponsors were space-fleet, missiles, physics, market, energy and science. Air and autofleet joined.

What it does. It makes the orbital war winnable. Three rungs sit on one shared debris record. Debris zones fade by altitude. They last twelve seconds in the stratosphere, forty in the thermosphere, and a hundred and twenty in space.

Rung one is Burst. Nukes split into blast, heat and electromagnetic pulse by air density. A high Starfish burst blinds the bombarder grid and zeroes its point defence in the zone. Starfish is a proposed burst type, from the physics council. It also hits your own unhardened drones and satellites, unless you research radhard. Hardened and sheltered aircraft are spared.

Rung two is Lane. The Retrograde Shotgun is a proposed weapon from the space-fleet council. It fires a pellet lane that cannot be intercepted and kills grid satellites. It can hit your own unhardened satellites.

Rung three is Burn. The Kessler Doctrine is an opt-in choice from the physics, space-fleet and market councils. It burns the whole thermosphere for a hundred eighty seconds, yours and theirs. Debris belts also shred re-entering pods.

A Halo is a laser satellite in the game today, with fifty health and a range of twelve hundred.

Size. Large.

The Speaker recommends the second wave.

### Ammunition and the One Locker. Fourteen votes.

Sponsors were kinetic-arms, railgun, industry, missiles and energy.

What it does. Ammunition Trains are a proposed supply system from the industry council. Guns near supply fire faster, and industrial capacity burns per shot. An empty stock means no bonus and no penalty. The Warhead Stockpile is proposed by the missiles council. It replaces the strike cooldown with magazine depth bought with industrial capacity.

Every kinetic gun gets a round selector. Choices include high-explosive, armor-piercing sabot, programmable airburst for flak, and guided rounds for rails. Shells finally get a blast radius.

Size. Medium to large. New research called smartfuze and apds.

The Speaker recommends the second wave.

### The Trunk. Nine votes.

Sponsors were industry, planet, command and railgun. Naval, ground, megaprojects and bio joined.

What it does. Rail Hubs within about twelve hundred pixels link into one buried network. A Rail Hub is a building in the game today. It is an underground rail node where idle troops dive in and redeploy fast. The network shares components, boosts linked industry, and rebuilds lost buildings through a Reconstruction Corps. That corps is a proposed crew from the industry council.

Things plug into it. The Molodets is a proposed strategic launcher that rides the rail, from the command council. There are harbour nodes. There are sally ports, where battalions debark behind a named line. A Launch Rail counts as a rail-linked Space Center. The Launch Rail is a proposed building from the railgun council. A Space Center is in the game today. It makes orbital units and gives five orbital lift. Upkeep is per thousand pixels of track.

Later, the network becomes the barrel of the Meridian Accelerator. That is a proposed doctrine from the railgun council. It turns your rail sprawl into one giant gun.

Why. It is the President's underground-railway seed.

Size. Large.

The Speaker recommends the second wave.

### Deep Deposits. Nine votes.

Sponsors were planet, industry, naval, science and bio.

What it does. Deposits get a grade and a depth. Extractors get five development tiers bought with industrial capacity. They raise yield and the strategic bonus. An Extractor is a building in the game today. You build it on a resource node. Deposits of the same type stack, which fixes the bug. Seabed nodules, seawater uranium and ocean thermal power give the navy an economy. Radiotroph Gardens are a proposed building from the bio council. They turn fallout into industrial capacity, influence and healing.

Size. Medium. New research called geosurvey.

The Speaker recommends the second wave.

### The Long Gun. Fifteen votes.

Sponsors were megaprojects, railgun, kinetic-arms and game feel. Physics and bio joined.

What it does. First, lift the Projects framework from an unfinished branch. It holds the Ledger of Projects. It has doctrines you fall into and megaprojects you build on purpose.

Then comes the Long Gun. It is a proposed superheavy gun from the megaprojects and railgun councils. It sits mid-map and turns. You build it section by section with no limit. Early sections are a light-gas supergun. Later sections are rail. Damage grows with section count. A hit interrupts the Orbital Annihilator's six-second beam charge. The Annihilator is an enemy superweapon in the game today. It has nine hundred fifty health and sits in space. The Long Gun's first shot in any twenty seconds gets a cinematic camera beat. The fleet sends Interdiction waves against the build site as heat rises.

Size. Extra large. New research called megastructures.

The Speaker recommends the second wave. It is the biggest build, and the one the President has wanted longest.

### One Net. Eight votes.

Sponsors were ground, kinetic-arms, space-fleet, air and missiles. Naval joined.

What it does. One engagement net and one solver replace the dead Fire Control. It is fed by counter-battery radar, the Argus plane, a relay satellite, silos and cruise missiles. The Argus is a proposed airborne radar plane from the air council. Sync Fire is optional. It lands everything in range in the same second, to overwhelm enemy point defence.

Size. Large. New research called firecontrol net.

### The Charter and the Decrees. Ten votes.

Sponsors were stability, lore, command and science.

What it does. Every dangerous technology gets a three-position dial. The settings are Restrain, Standing and Unleash. This makes the hidden limit a visible choice. Decrees spend stability for tempo. They are Haste, Reprisal and Evacuation. A capitulation menu includes Continuity of Government.

Size. Medium.

The Speaker recommends the second wave. It is good, but heavy on screens and less urgent than the fleet and the economy.

### The Navigable Moat. Nine votes.

Sponsors were planet, naval and ground. A free canal brush connects seas with canals and locks. A chain of sluice gates can flood a valley, with a ten-second evacuation warning. Enemy ground landings that cannot swim drown. The fleet answers with Trench Landers, a proposed enemy from the naval council that lands at sea level and builds hunters. So the sea finally gets a front. Sappers are proposed combat engineers from the ground council. Delta Works seawalls, from the planet council, let you hold land below sea level. Size large.

### Dispersal. Six votes.

Sponsors were missiles, naval and air. It builds the launchers the project notes promised. A land mobile launcher and sea launchers share one warhead stock. Boomer submarines, proposed by the missiles council, sit in a new depth band and hide from bombers. The Arsenal Ship Leviathan is in the game today, with fourteen hundred range. Air gets a dipping-sonar Kite and sonobuoys. The Kite is an attack helicopter in the game today. The sonar version is proposed. Size medium to large.

### The Power Grid. Three votes.

Sponsors were energy, railgun, space-fleet, physics and science. One power pool is fed by Power Plants and Helios solar satellites. A Power Plant is in the game today and gives seven industrial capacity per second. The Helios is a proposed solar satellite from the space-fleet council, so orbit has something worth defending. Capacitor banks let rails overdrive, with bore wear as a real cost. A shortage becomes industrial debt, never a throttle. Size medium.

### The Dossier Line. Four votes.

Sponsors were science, lore and industry. Enemy wrecks yield samples and black boxes. Samples cut matching research costs, and each dossier gives a permanent damage bonus against that enemy kind. The fleet answers with Sanitiser drones, a proposed enemy from the industry council, that detonate wrecks. Size medium.

### The Fleet Will Return. Eleven votes.

Sponsors were pacing, autofleet, game feel and lore.

What it does. Withdrawal becomes an Interbellum lasting forty-five percent of preparation, with a stability reset. A stronger second fleet returns. It opens with a gambit drawn from what killed it last time. The gambit is Net, FOBS or Wedge. It fields Mirror echoes of your top-killing unit. The Mirror is a proposed enemy idea from the autofleet council. The final withdrawal gets the full cinematic.

Size. Large.

The Speaker holds this one for the President. See the open questions.

## Smaller bills with cross-council support

These had no vote totals listed.

Also in this group are The Breathing Sky, The Wire as Home Front, The Exercise and a game feel set including Spin-Up. The Living Battalion, from ground, bio and command. Squads pool into battalions that regrow on manpower and become Shaken at forty percent strength.

Synthesis Doctrines, from science, with five votes. Research combos fire permanent world rules, not just units.

Orion, from space-fleet and megaprojects. A three-stage megaproject that ends in a nuclear-pulse capital ship. The Orion Battleship is proposed by the space-fleet council.

## Hot takes worth remembering

Seven ideas were not carried. They include a reverse-engineered warp catapult, a Titan larva that never stops growing, and a capital that walks on treads.

## The Speaker's recommendation

Sign first the core that deepens the game without bloating it.

One. The Thermal Field. It tops the ballot, it is the President's own seed, and it gives every weapon family a reason to exist.

Two. The Fleet Reads You, with The Nerve. Five wins with one doctrine is the clearest problem in the reports. These fix it with a smarter, scarier enemy, not a weaker air force. The Nerve also gives the space fleet a win condition.

Three. The One Forecast and The Standing Plan. They are the two slices the President already planned. They fix the unexplained invasion and the hoard.

Quick fixes to sign with them. Bake evicted terrain edits instead of undoing them. Make deposits stack. Cache the audio noise buffer. Add Spin-Up.

Second wave. Dead Sky Protocol, so space matters. Ammunition and the One Locker. Deep Deposits. The Trunk. The Long Gun with the Projects framework.

Where the Speaker overrules a popular bill. The Fleet Will Return had eleven votes. It is held for the President, because it turns a victory into an intermission. That is a decision about what a win means. The Charter goes in the second wave.

## Four questions only the President can answer

First. The Shapeshifter damage slump. Psi wants it back. The Chair struck it.

Second. The Standing Plan overhead. Is it a six percent tax, or four industrial capacity per line?

Third. Is the information policy dial allowed?

Fourth. Should a second siege exist at all? Does a win end the match, or start an intermission?
