# Lore Notes: Calamity discussions with Eve

Persistent memory for the Calamity lore threads in Eve's main chat. If she just brought lore back up, read section 9 first.

**Why this file exists.** The lore talk began on 11-10, when Eve was annoyed that Silva is treated as evil and asked what she ever did to earn it. It grew into a long, layered conversation about gods and morality. The 11-10 thread was later lost when the conversation was summarised, and afterwards Claude nodded along instead of checking what Eve was referring to. This file is the fix.

**Tags.** CANON = the mod's own text, as reported in the thread (reverify exact wording before relying on it). EVE = Eve's idea or reading. PITCH = the assistant's invention or interpretation, offered to her. UNSURE = source unclear, or it survives only as a topic heading.

**Where the 11-10 to 11-12 material comes from.** The live transcript starts on 11-12, so the early thread survives only in three places:
- the compaction summary at the top of the transcript;
- the dated lore lines in `ROADMAP.md` (11-10 to 11-13);
- `warbands-mod/docs/LORE_FACTIONS.md`, deleted later but still in git (`git show 1a60dea:warbands-mod/docs/LORE_FACTIONS.md`). It holds the assistant's 11-10 answers to Eve's three opening questions.

**Voice-to-text names.** Yaram, Yarim, Yeren, Yerem, Yoram, Urim = Yharim. Drayden = Draedon. Sylvia, Silvia = Silva. Azaferd, Azophyr = Azafure. Elnaris = Ilmeris. Amida, Midas = Amidias (the Sea King). Anita = Anahita. Glorified = chlorophyte. Mexican invasion = Martians.

## 1. The Silva question

**How it started (11-10).** Eve asked: what did Silva actually do, in the lore, to be counted as evil? She asked three things alongside it: was Yharim justified, why burn the sea kingdom, and who survives in the world afterwards.

**What the text accuses her of: nothing specific.** (CANON, 11-10 answer)
- She is the goddess of life. Yharim killed her and dumped her remains in the Abyss.
- His case against her is categorical, not personal. Once Xeroc ate Zeratros and the other gods copied him, every god became complicit in the dragons' genocide, whatever their intentions. So Silva is guilty because she is a god, not because of anything she did.
- Crabulon's entry hints at Yharim's personal disgust for life that won't let the dead rest. That disgust colours how he writes about her remains.
- Her remains refused to fully die. They kept growing, pushed Anahita out of the Abyss, keep the Sulphurous Sea's life going, and burrowed toward the underworld.

**Eve's case for her innocence ("my goat").**
- Blossom Flux, the legendary bow, carries Silva's own message to Plantera. (CANON) This was Eve's vindication on 11-12: "I FUCKING KNEW IT… SHE WAS NICE." She raised it again on 11-17.
- A world-generation step blesses a flourishing jungle grove. (CANON)
- She blessed the first Primordial Wyrm long before the war. (CANON)
- Life Magic, the Dryad's art, was pioneered by Silva. (CANON)
- She gives life fruit and life crystals. Eve wrote her own lines on this. (EVE) In-game, a "Life Fruit detected nearby!" readout shows in the Vernal Pass.
- The Abyss is a "reimprisonment". (CANON)
- Her remains imply the Devourer did not kill her. (CANON reading, 11-12)
- No line accuses her. Braelor fought Yharim, but to Yharim every god was guilty. (CANON)
- Eve asked the Calamity Discord about Silva years ago. Her instinct then was the same. (EVE)

**What happened to her (CANON).**
- She was killed somewhere else, and Yharim disposed of her remains in the Abyss. She never lived there.
- The wyrms were already hiding in the Abyss from the gods' purges, so her body landed beside the kin she had protected.
- Her roots tore down through the Abyss stone into the underworld. Azafure, built beneath it, fed on what dripped through.
- So she died before Azafure fell. Why she was killed is never stated.

**Analysis from 11-12 (PITCH unless tagged).**
- Silva is absent from the God-King scheme. A pro-dragon goddess wouldn't sign on to having Yharim eat Yharon, and Yharon is the one who revives auric dragons (CANON). So she may already have been sidelined, or imprisoned.
- Voidstone may be why the Abyss works as her prison.
- The dark irony: Yharim dumped her right on top of his best ally, Azafure.

**Eve's theories (EVE).**
- **The darkness is deliberate (11-17).** Abyss torch tooltips, such as the thermal and void torches, talk about cleansing magical darkness from the Abyss. Natural darkness wouldn't need cleansing, so Silva darkened it on purpose to shelter the wyrms. That would also explain the 380,000-health guardians: the Abyss is a sanctuary meant to be unreachable. The assistant agreed it's a strong clue.
- **She left to protect her own (11-17).** She came out to stop Yharim burning her champion's home and her people, and was killed for it. This is Eve's current favourite. Earlier she weighed four timeline options, A to D (lost, UNSURE). She also asked whether Silva went into hiding out of fear, and whether she "landed at the bottom" of the Abyss.
- **"Shaped in her image."** Yharim taunts that the Abyss now bears her image and calls it the worst fate for such a pit. Eve's answer: it's full of life now, and if that life is grim, that's his doing.
- **The villagers made the chemical weapons, not Silva.** The Vernal Pass shackled souls to make Plantera as a guardian against Yharim (CANON). Eve's reading: the villagers did it out of fear, and Yharim projects hubris onto desperate people. On 11-15, mid-rage at Plantera's spore gas, she joked that she didn't feel sorry for the Vernal Pass any more and that Silva would have used anthrax. She took the Silva part back straight away.
- **Silva was gifted her soul,** rather than taking it the way the other gods did. (EVE) In a world where godhood means eating a dragon, that is a moral distinction, not a detail.
- Eve built a Silva shrine in her own world. (EVE)

**Design.** Open. Eve wants something partly human, not the generic woman made of leaves. PITCH: her apparent age shifts with the season or hour; a heart motif in life-crystal red; a body that is an ecosystem others depend on.

**Silva armor.** Dragonfolly feathers, Planty Mush and Ascendant Spirit Essence (fused souls), made at the Cosmic Anvil. Mage and summoner heads only. Read as lore on 11-12; Eve hoped to rush it on 11-17.

## 2. Gods and morality

The core of the conversation. Calamity's lore items are narrated by Yharim himself, so a recurring theme is how far to trust the narrator.

**Are gods guilty by what they are?**
- Yharim's position: yes. The whole god system rests on eating dragon souls, and the gods hunted the dragons and purged their kin. Every god is a sinner by office. (CANON)
- Eve's position: that is guilt by category, and Silva shows the gap. Nothing she did is ever named, she protected dragon-kin, and her soul may have been a gift. (EVE)
- The double standard: Yharim spares Astrum Deus as a kind of wrongful conviction, infected rather than guilty. He never extends that mercy to Silva or Ilmeris. (11-12 analysis, PITCH built on CANON) Eve asked why he'd kill pro-dragon Silva but spare Deus. On 11-13 she also said sparing Deus was fair, since Deus is a different kind of being ("mostly a worm"). (EVE)

**Is Yharim justified?** The assistant's 11-10 verdict: a crusader with a real grievance who became a tyrant. (PITCH) The evidence:
- **The grievance is real.** He refused to eat Yharon's soul and become God-King, scorning destiny, and was executed for it by being thrown into lava with the egg. (CANON) The assistant called him "a martyr who didn't stay dead". (11-10)
- **He criticises himself (CANON):**
  - He calls the Corruption and Crimson his first mistake: dead gods' essence left to rot.
  - Apathy let the Dungeon's prisoners become the Polterghast.
  - Recruiting the Devourer is where he says his descent began.
  - Permafrost left him because, in Permafrost's eyes, justice had turned into tyranny.
- **But there may be no "fall".** Calamitas (Gruesome Eminence) says he never fell from grace. The assistant dropped its "decline" reading because of that line. (CANON) It also revised "performed regret" to sincere grief after the Yharon entry, which describes him as a husk. (CANON, 11-12)
- **Rancor.** Yharim lied that Otonilou had unleashed the Brimstone Elemental, which turned Calamitas against Ilmeris. (CANON, wiki) Eve: "I also got it right that yharim was a dick", and later, "I really hate him now."
- **The apathy pattern (EVE).** A creature turns into an evil weapon, Yharim says "oh no" and does nothing. She called it a pattern. A related "wall-punching pattern" came up; its details are lost. (UNSURE)
- **Unreliable narrator (EVE + PITCH, 11-12).** Yharim projects hubris onto desperate people. The Vernal Pass acted from fear, not pride.
- **Questions Eve raised (11-12, UNSURE how far they got):**
  - Did Yharim plan the war long before the refusal? Did he secretly want Ilmeris destroyed? ("Ideological reasons ruin the war, part 748.")
  - Was he waiting for a successor, using people as stepping stones? Draedon says Yharim expected to die a natural death. (CANON)
  - Was he manipulating a child, presumably Calamitas? She compared him to Napoleon ("Napoleon was based").
  - Did he win?

**Ilmeris: victim or obstacle?**
- Eve sees it as an unjust victim. (EVE)
- The text makes the burning strategic. Ilmeris never expanded, but its gods electrified its waters so no one could cross, and its Ghost Bells and trained sand sharks held his armies back. Only the Brimstone Witch could break that line, which she did in the Great Incineration. That act is probably the "horrific sin" Calamitas carries. (CANON plus 11-10 reading)
- The Ghost Bell entry covers the war; Yharim's underwater Arsenal was never deployed. (CANON)

**Azafure.**
- Yharim did not burn it. The Brimstone Elemental woke, whether from over-mining, from pollution, or from a deliberate plot (rumours, CANON). Calamitas, who grew up there and whose brothers were Azafurians, fought the Elemental to a standstill, and the city burned in between. (CANON)
- Calamitas's power traces back to the Elemental. (CANON, Draedon)
- The Underworld entry hints that the gods once burned Azafure for refusing them. (CANON) The forgemasters binding the Elemental may have triggered its waking. (PITCH)
- Eve read Azafure's fall as sabotage, and the Elemental fight as a "skill issue". (EVE)

**Tyrants or protectors? The gods vary.**
- The dictator deities ran a surveillance theocracy that burned contrary evidence. Eve: "these people really suck." (CANON + EVE)
- The gods tried to rule Azafure. (CANON)
- Providence promised to end inequality by reducing everyone to featureless ash, and her worshippers saw little value in life. (CANON)
- The Onyx Kinsmen worship the Slime God for keeping balance. (CANON)
- Ilmeris was a constitutional monarchy overseen by gods. Eve asked how that works with god-tier monarchs, and compared Ilmeris to Belgium in 1914. (EVE) PITCH: real but limited kings, with the gods as distant patrons.
- The gods banned necromancy, and the God of Sacrifices' cult broke that ban to make the Ravager. (CANON) Eve also raised necromancy and resurrection in general (11-12).

**The soul system as a moral question.**
- Godhood is literally made by eating a dragon. There were only so many auric souls, so the gods were a closed club built on a genocide. (CANON + PITCH)
- The God-King plan was the gods drafting their most dangerous hero into the club. PITCH: they drafted champions into godhood to keep strong mortals inside the system, which is why Yharim saying no was so threatening.
- The Eidolists, the former Dragon Cult, serve a "new master" in the Abyss. (CANON)

**Does power decide morality?**
- Champions are rare and work like nuclear weapons; Calamitas is the setting's nuke. (PITCH, 11-12)
- Eve asked how ordinary people live in a world of such different power levels. Part of the game's power fantasy is killing power-drunk gods. (EVE, 11-13)
- The victor narrates: almost all the moral framing comes from Yharim's own lore items.
- Eve's self-aware "victim-blaming" joke: the Vernal Pass should have beaten Plantera with twenty people and grappling hooks. (EVE)

**Draedon's morality.**
- He builds superweapons for whoever pays. (PITCH) He experiments without consent: the Calamitas clone, biological weapons. (EVE) He rates his own weapons harshly in their descriptions, such as the Augur. Eve: "an asshole, but he earned it"; he designed about eight bosses. (EVE)
- **The Plague.** Draedon's account says Yharim freed the first Plague specimen in a rage. (CANON) The 11-10 summary says Yharim broke with Draedon over the enslaved Plague bees. (CANON per 11-10 doc)
- Draedon dumped nuclear waste in the already-ruined Sulphurous Sea, and the public revolted. (CANON) Eve: never let Yharim have a broom, and why not use the Clentaminator? (EVE joke)
- 11-17: plague "nukes" are biological weapons fired from mind-controlled bees. That makes Draedon the supplier to every side, which explains Yharim's anger and why everyone was losing. (EVE)

## 4. The world at the war's start (1781), reference

Compiled during the passing preset idea in section 8. CANON facts, PITCH fills.
- **Yharim and Yharon:** a traitor and a phoenix beside Azafure, with no state yet. (CANON) Executed near Azafure, so the city watched him rise from the lava. (PITCH)
- **Permafrost:** early ally. He guided conquests and later governed cities. Home is the snow biome. (CANON)
- **Azafure:** the oldest and largest city, in the underworld beneath the Abyss. Guns, smithing, magic, and brimstone exported everywhere. The Elemental sleeps beneath it. It was at war with the demon clans, which have no lord. One of the first to back Yharim. (CANON) Guild-run and godless, 100,000 to 300,000 people, trade shafts to the surface. (PITCH)
- **Dictator deities:** the future evil biome. (CANON)
- **Ilmeris:** the whole desert is the Ilmeran Sea, and the Sunken Sea its countryside. It has Otonilou and the God of the Seas, coral cities, "brutish" neighbours, and Amidias as its 17th ruler, not necessarily reigning yet. (CANON) A sand seabed; the neighbours covet sea prisms. (PITCH)
- **Vernal Pass:** Silva alive, Braelor, a route to the Lihzahrd temple. (CANON) Eve's screenshots show a tree island with two unbloomed Giant Plantera Bulbs and a small unwalled farming village with boomsticks. That fits her "they were growing more than one Plantera" theory, and "Plantera bulbs as an army". (EVE)
- **Jungle:** abandoned Lihzahrds, bee villages, Dragonfollies. (CANON)
- **Abyss:** a bare trench, home to Anahita, the hidden wyrms, the Old Duke and the Aquatic Scourge. The Sulphurous Sea is polluted but not yet irradiated. (CANON)
- **Dungeon:** the Dragon Cult's fortress and library; its ritual keeps the Moon Lord sealed; Yharim calls them former friends. (CANON)
- **Sky:** Chrysadia, under the Storm Dragon, later reduced to remnants. (CANON) Alive but fading. (PITCH)
  - Moonstone Crown flavour (CANON, paraphrased; Eve found it 11-19): Chrysadia was mourned for its "marring": one dusk its clouds parted not onto the stars it loved or its patron gods, but onto a hungry void and a dread that would not lift. The crown is crafted from the Feather Crown (a sky-island item), so feathers → Chrysadia, moonstone → its night sky.
  - Cross-references (CANON): the Storm Weaver lore says Chrysadia knew that serpent for eating wyverns whole, and that it came into the world through a rift the Devourer opened; wyverns still guard Chrysadia's ruins for the Storm Dragon; harpies settled the sky islands only after the old civilization fell.
  - Reading (PITCH): Chrysadia fell to an attack from the sky, most likely the Devourer's rift and its serpents (the Storm Weaver, maybe the Ceaseless Void as the literal 'void'), while its patron, the Storm Dragon, didn't come. Fits Eve's earlier 'Storm: Chrysadia's god or its destroyer' and answers open question 8 (Chrysadia no longer stands by the present). Resonance, not canon: Wrath of the Gods' rift eclipse is the same image, a sky swallowed by a void.
- **Forest:** unowned in the text. God-ruled human kingdoms. (PITCH)
- **Onyx Kinsmen:** on the surface. (PITCH)
- **Empty land:** monsters spawn wherever there are no walls, so people live in walled towns. (EVE)
- **Not yet existing:** Calamitas, the Wall of Flesh, Draedon's labs, the Plague, the Astral Infection, Plantera, the overgrown Abyss, the desert.

## 5. Gods, reference

- **Rule (CANON):** every god runs on one auric dragon soul and holds its aspect. The souls were finite, so there are tens of gods. The assistant guesses 10 to 30. (PITCH) Named domains: Life, Death, Flame, Storm, Light, Rebirth, Minds, Sunlight.
- **Canon gods:**
  - Xeroc (Light, hidden).
  - Providence (Flame; one of four heroes). Eve thinks she gave up her human form.
  - Silva (Life).
  - Otonilou and the God of the Seas.
  - The Slime God. Eve thinks Yharim purged it.
  - The God of Sacrifices.
  - The dictator deities.
  - The Lihzahrds' lost god.
  - An unnamed god inside the Wall of Flesh.
- **Probably not soul-gods:** Astrum Deus; the Brimstone Elemental (who has sisters) and Anahita; the Empress of Light.
- **Appearance:** some gods were people and some never were. None shown on screen is fully human.
- **Pitched gods (PITCH):**
  - Minds: the dictator gods.
  - Death: the God of Sacrifices.
  - Sunlight: the Lihzahrds' god, with Golem rebuilt from memory of it.
  - Storm: Chrysadia's god, or Otonilou as its destroyer.
  - Crimson shrine: hunger and blood.
  - Marble shrine: glory and contests.
  - Desert shrine (the Osiris joke): tombs.
  - Mushroom biome: rot.
  - Granite shrine: the miners.
  - Shimmer: transformation.
  - Ice shrine: winter beasts.
  - Wall of Flesh: a light god whose essence becomes the Hallow. Eve says a lore doc calls the Wall an alchemical block on the souls of light. (UNSURE)

## 6. Other topics

- **Martians:** almost no Calamity lore. The probe senses the planet's growing power; the saucer core says otherworldly creatures destroyed their world. (CANON) They read as refugees.
- **Nukes.**
  - The original "nukes in Terraria" talk predates the transcript. Only a recap survives: the Skyfin Bomber, the spent nuclear fuel rod and the Plague Nuke, discussed as nukes being a functional tool, plus acid-rain nukes. Eve recalls the conclusion as "it would probably work, but it's a bit much", and she pushed back that fission bombs would work. (UNSURE: details lost)
  - 17 Nov: plague nukes are biological weapons, not nuclear, fired from mind-controlled bees, which makes Draedon the arms dealer to everyone. (EVE)
  - Calamity's "nukes" are mostly big homing explosives by name: Ravager, Magnomaly Cannon, Ares, Plaguebringer. (CANON names)
- **Doomsday Device:** an alien quantum bomb that you just throw, like Excalibur still stuck in its rock. (EVE joke) The "activate it" idea sits in the idea pile only.
- **Golem's respect:** Yharim mocks Golem all game, yet it outclasses his mechs, though not the Exo Mechs. Gameplay or lore? Eve also offered a screenshot of the trash talk (11-12). (EVE)
- **Fitting new vanilla bosses in:** Eve remembers the Slime God dying, then teleporting away, then Queen Slime arriving. Calamity patches new bosses in with a flavour line.
- **Gods hinted at in gear and structures:** Eve thinks weapons, armour and underground structures point to more gods. Not researched yet.
- **Xeroc vs the Primordial Wyrm:** they never fought. The Wyrm would have attacked him and lost; it surpassed nearly all its kin except Zeratros. (CANON) Eve had a theory about bonding with the Wyrm. (EVE, lost) She also raised Eidolists and Eidolon tablets.
- **Draedon's Codebreaker conversation:** a key dated source, missed until Eve pointed it out on 11-12.
- **Wrath of the Gods** is not Calamity canon. (EVE)
