# Boss AI against an army: what Calamity's late bosses really do, and how they could fight an army well

Lab `bossai`, 2026-11-23. Read in Calamity 2.2.2's source (the lab's copy), then checked against Eve's 2.2.4 (the Workshop build for tML 2026.6, decompiled): every code path named below is the same in 2.2.4, only the line numbers move. Line numbers here are 2.2.2's. Levy 3.1.12 + this branch.

## In plain words

The short version is that most of what looks "naive" about the late bosses against your army is not Calamity being naive. Calamity wrote these bosses to fight one to a few players, and in several places it already knows how to fight several targets at once. The trouble is the way Levy points a boss at a soldier: for the length of that one boss's own thinking step, a stand-in for the soldier is put into the one "no player" slot of the game (slot 255), and taken out again right after. Anything that looks at the boss's target from somewhere else (an arm, a vent, a missile in flight, a scan for "every player near me") never sees a soldier. It sees an empty slot, or it sees only you.

That has three big effects on the Exo Mechs, and all three were measured. Ares's four arms aim at the empty slot, which sits in the top-left corner of the world: in every lab fight, 100% of the time his arms were looking about 1,900 tiles away from the nearest soldier, so his tesla orbs, plasma fireballs and Gauss Nukes fly off toward the corner and almost never touch anyone (tesla orbs 2%, plasma fireballs 5%, Gauss Nukes 0% of the time). Ares's damage against your army comes almost entirely from the two attacks that don't aim at anybody: the spinning Blenderbeam and the Exothermal Laser sweep. Thanatos's vents fire at "up to three players within 150 tiles", which is Calamity's own multiplayer attack, but the army isn't players, so his vents fire at you or at nobody: 3% of his 1,372 lasers touched a soldier, and his whole underground laser phase is six seconds of him lying under the floor doing nothing to the army (he was under the floor 66-84% of his time). Apollo's rockets and the Gauss Nuke home on the nearest *player*, so they curve toward you wherever you stand.

The second, quieter effect is that the bosses never commit to a target. Calamity treats slot 255 as "no target", so every Calamity boss fighting the army searches for a new target every single tick, and the mechs changed which soldier they were hunting 7 to 35 times a minute. Calamity's own code picks a target at the start of each attack and keeps it (the Devourer of Gods even has a comment about not retargeting constantly). And because each mech's stand-in is its own, the Exo Mechs stop working as a team: Calamity makes every mech share Ares's target (so the twins flank one victim from both sides and Ares hangs above it), but against the army they shared a target only 0-26% of the time.

My proposal is to fix the first effect for every boss as plain parity (a boss's arms and missiles should see what its body sees, and "every player near me" should include soldiers), and then make a few deliberate, code-grounded choices about how a boss fights a crowd: area weapons aim at where soldiers are bunched, single-target weapons keep focus, bosses commit to a target per attack instead of per tick, a pair like the twins keeps one shared victim, and an enrage that punishes a player for running away should not fire because one soldier happens to stand outside the arena. Nothing here makes a boss stronger by number tweaks, and none of it touches your own fight when you're solo.

I built the first three for the Exo Mechs behind a new setting, "Bosses adapt to armies" (off by default; it only does anything while a beaten mech is fighting a fielded army). With all three on, your army was wiped in 42-55 seconds instead of 84-100, and the mechs kept 73-81% of their life instead of 39-51% (three seeds per opener, so the direction is solid and the exact sizes are not). The arms and missiles alone change much less (the mechs kept 55-77%) and cost nothing extra; Thanatos's vents are the big step, and they cost about 3.6 ms of server time per tick, from the three times as many lasers. I would not turn it on by default without you trying it: it makes Ares and Thanatos genuinely dangerous to the army for the first time, and your army already loses the Exo fight in the lab. Details and tables are in the Prototype section.

## How Levy points a boss at the army today

This is the mechanism every finding below depends on, so it's worth being exact.

`EnemyTargeting` (Levy/Common/Targeting/EnemyTargeting.cs) lets a beaten boss (`BossAggroUnlocked`: the boss type is in `BossDownedSystem`, and "Bosses target soldiers" is on) choose a levy instead of a player. After any target search (`NPC.TargetClosest`, and Calamity's own `CalamityUtils.CalamityTargeting` through the IL hook in `CalamityTargetingCompat`), `CompeteCore` (line 414) compares the best levy against the player the game picked, then `AggroSpread.Pick` takes the least crowded levy among those close to the best one. The boss's `npc.target` becomes 255, and `EnemyTargetingGlobalNPC.Prepare` (line 965) puts the chosen levy's stand-in `Player` into `Main.player[255]` for that NPC's own AI call; `PostAI` takes it out. Everything inside the boss's AI that reads `Main.player[NPC.target]` therefore sees the soldier.

What the swap does not reach is everything outside that one AI call, and four families of Calamity code live there. A boss PART that reads its body's target from its own AI (`Main.npc[body].target`, which is 255) reads slot 255 outside the swap: vanilla's empty player at (0, 0). Levy already fixes this for vanilla bosses with `LendBossLock` (line 1035: Skeletron's hands, Plantera's hooks), but only for the vanilla roots in `BossRule.MainBossSet`; a Calamity part that is a `realLife` follower (`IsFollowerSegment`) never searches itself and never gets a lend. A PROJECTILE that searches by itself (`Player.FindClosest`) sees players only; `FlyerSlotProjectile` and `EventProjectileAim` cover a handful of vanilla cases. A MULTI-TARGET SCAN (`foreach (Player plr in Main.ActivePlayers)`) sees players only. And an ENRAGE OR DESPAWN RULE that reads the target's position now reads a soldier's position, which can trip it for reasons that have nothing to do with the player running away.

Two more things matter for Calamity specifically. Every Calamity boss I read starts its AI with `if (NPC.target < 0 || NPC.target == Main.maxPlayers || ...) NPC.TargetClosest();` (AresBody 519, ThanatosHead 308, Apollo 245, Artemis 278, SupremeCalamitas 517, Providence 399, Yharon 319, DevourerofGodsHead 436, the brothers, the Sepulcher, the Soul Seekers). `Main.maxPlayers` is 255, so a boss locked on the army re-runs its target search on every tick, and Levy's `Compete` answers it every tick with only `LockStickinessPx = 50` px of loyalty to the current lock. And the boss rule's "spite" re-aiming (`BossRuleGlobalProjectile`) only ever applies to the vanilla main bosses, so none of the Calamity bosses here have their shots redirected by Levy; what they hit is entirely Calamity's own aim.

Bodyguard (Common/Movement/Bodyguard.cs) and the crowding weights move LEVIES (the targeted levy draws the boss away; the others keep off the lane), so they change where the boss goes but not how it aims; they are left as they are here.

## The Exo Mechs

### Draedon and who fights when

Draedon himself decides nothing in the fight. `Draedon.cs` follows `Main.player[NPC.target]` (196-212; he despawns if no living target exists), plays the music and says the phase lines when a mech writes his `localAI[0]`. The real controller is spread over the mechs: each reads the others' life ratios through Calamity's static slots (`CalamityGlobalNPC.draedonExoMechPrime`, `...Worm`, `...TwinGreen`) and moves itself between its states. The first mech fights alone until 70% (AresBody 280-310, ThanatosHead "Spawn the other mechs if Thanatos is first", Apollo 375), then summons the other two on its target (`NPC.SpawnOnPlayer(player.whoAmI, ...)`, which is slot 255's stand-in through the swap) and goes passive and immune; a mech returns when another drops below 70% (AresBody 387); a mech below 40% goes berserk and the others leave (`otherMechIsBerserk`, AresBody 326-341); after one dies the remaining ones return. None of this reads a player except the summon position, so the phase logic works the same against an army. Lab phases matched Calamity's order in every fight.

The one thing Draedon's design does assume about targets is that the mechs hunt together. Thanatos copies Ares's target when Ares is alive (`CheckForOtherMechs`, ThanatosHead 258-268), Apollo copies Thanatos's and then Ares's (Apollo 270-292), Artemis copies Apollo's, Thanatos's and Ares's (Artemis 286-325). So in Calamity all three mechs always converge on one victim: Ares 425 px above it (AresBody 599), Artemis 750 px to its left and Apollo 750 px to its right at its height (Apollo 472-480, Artemis 555-561), Thanatos charging through it. Against the army each mech reads index 255 inside its own AI, where its own stand-in is, so each mech hunts its own soldier. OBSERVED: the open mechs shared one target 0-26% of the time (median per opener 13, 0, 26%).

### XF-09 Ares and his arms

Ares's body (AresBody.cs) hovers above his target (destination `player.Center - (0, 425)`, line 599), and every so often enters the Deathray phase: he flies to within 480 px of the target (`deathrayDistanceGateValue`, 629 and 876), telegraphs and fires the Blenderbeam, 10 rays on Death spinning around his own centre (`totalProjectiles = death ? 10 : ...`, 903-994). That attack is radial and hits whoever is around him; it doesn't care how many targets there are, which is why it is the attack that works best against your army. One part of it reads the target: if the target is more than `DeathrayEnrageDistance = 2480` px from him once the phase has started (883-897), he enrages for the rest of it (DR 85%, speed 22). With the per-tick retarget, a switch to a far soldier mid-spin trips it. OBSERVED: Ares was enraged 0-11% of the time (Thanatos-first fights 11%).

The arms are the big one. Every arm reads `int targetIndex = Main.npc[CalamityGlobalNPC.draedonExoMechPrime].target;` (AresLaserCannon 208, AresGaussNuke 202, AresTeslaCannon 201, AresPlasmaFlamethrower 200) and aims at `Main.player[targetIndex]`, with prediction on three of them (`Main.player[targetIndex].velocity * predictionAmt`: Gauss 15, tesla 30, plasma 15 on Death). The arms are `realLife` followers of the body (`Main.npc[lol].realLife = NPC.whoAmI`, AresBody 345), so Levy's `Prepare` skips them (`ShouldPollForSoldier` is false for a follower) and `LendBossLock` doesn't apply to a Calamity root: in an arm's AI call slot 255 is vanilla's empty player. OBSERVED in every lab fight: 100% of arm AI calls read the empty slot, and the point the arms aim at was 1,868-1,968 tiles from the nearest levy (the corner of the world). The world check reproduces it with a row of levies (`[ExoFix] ARMS, feature off`). What that does to each arm follows from its projectile. The Laser Cannon's normal shots go at the corner; its Exothermal Laser deathray sweeps straight down or sideways regardless of target (`horizontalLaserSweep`, AresLaserCannon 273-276), so it still lands, and it is the top named killer in the lab (17% of deaths). The tesla orbs fly toward the corner and chain to each other (2% touched a levy). The plasma fireballs fly to the target point `(ai0, ai1)` and burst into bolts there (AresPlasmaFlamethrower 451): the corner, 5%. The Gauss Nuke is fired at the corner with `ai[1] = target Y`, then steers by itself (next paragraph); 0 of 25 touched a levy and its explosion caught soldiers only when its timer ran out over the army (24%).

Two of Calamity's projectiles pick their own target: the Gauss Nuke projectile (`int target = Player.FindClosest(Projectile.Center, 1, 1)`, AresGaussNukeProjectile 121; it homes until within 280 px and explodes on contact) and Apollo's rockets (ApolloRocket 129; home until 160 px, then accelerate). Both see players only. With you standing in the army they home on you; in the lab, with you 150 tiles away, they curve away toward you and time out over whatever is below.

### XM-05 Thanatos

The head (ThanatosHead.cs) alternates a Charge (it steers at the target with growing speed, 760-797), an Underground Laser Barrage (it moves to `destination + (0, ±800)`, 50 tiles below or above the target, alternating by `NPC.ai[1] % 2`, line 515, and waits there for 4.5-6 s, 801-846), and in berserk a Deathray (it closes to 600 px and fires a six-telegraph beam, 849-965). The segments do the shooting. In both the Charge and the Barrage, opening segments run `foreach (Player plr in Main.ActivePlayers)` and collect up to `maxTargets = 3` players within `maxDistance = 2400` px (ThanatosBody1 276-307 in Charge, 337-394 in Barrage), then fire a laser at each, and in the Barrage a predictive and an "opposite" laser too (`targetCenter ± velocity * predictionAmt`). This is a deliberately multiplayer attack: it is Calamity spreading Thanatos's fire across a team. Against the army the scan finds only you. OBSERVED: 1,372 Thanatos lasers in 9 fights, 3% touched a levy (the ones that crossed the army on the way to you); Thanatos's head was more than 3 tiles under the arena floor 66-84% of the time. With you out of his 2400 px scan (the world check), his vents fire nothing at all. INFERRED, but it follows directly: in your real fights, where you stand in the army, every vent laser goes at you.

### XS-01 Artemis and XS-03 Apollo

The twins hover on opposite sides of their (shared, in Calamity) target and attack it: Artemis's laser shots and laser shotgun with prediction (`predictionAmt` 20, x1.5 for the shotgun, Artemis 508-516), her Deathray spin, which sweeps from a fixed side (top, bottom, left, right by `ai[3]`, 983-1010), Apollo's plasma fireballs aimed at the target point (Apollo 997), rockets (`FindClosest`, above), and the charge combo, a four-leg zig-zag of 97-105 px/tick charges laid out from his position relative to the target (1081-1193). The spin and the zig-zag cover space rather than a person, so they work on an army. What the army loses is the pincer: with each twin on its own soldier they don't flank anyone. The twins also mirror their life (Artemis 300-302; Levy's scaling already handles that since exofixes).

## Supreme Witch Calamitas and her court

SCal (SupremeCalamitas.cs, 3,800 lines) fights from the centre of a 2000-2500 px arena box she makes at the start (`safeBox`, 629-670), alternating five attack sections and five bullet hells. Her aimed attacks read `player` = `Main.player[NPC.target]`: hover shots, dashes, hellblasts and gigablasts, with prediction on the brimstone bottles (`player.Center + player.velocity * 20f`, 1080, 1412, 1595). The bullet hells are laid out around the target's position, not hers: blasts from 1000 px above the target, from 1000 px either side (`player.position.X ± 1000`, 964-990). Her enrage reads the target too: if the arena box exists and her target is outside it, she gets 99.99% unbreakable DR and her projectiles speed up to 2x (`!Collision.CheckAABBvAABBCollision(ArenaBox..., player...)`, 813-826). Calamity's arena box only holds players (`ArenaWallPlayer`); Levy's `ArenaBoxes.Contain` holds levies that are inside it, so a levy left outside stays outside.

Her brothers Cataclysm and Catastrophe flank her target from opposite sides (`Target.Center + new Vector2(HorizontalOffset * (targetSide ? -1 : 1), VerticalOffset)`, SupremeCataclysm 260-307) and punch at it; against the army each brother hunts its own soldier, so the flank is lost exactly as with the twins. The Sepulcher doesn't hunt anyone: it circles SCal (`targetX = SCal's position`, SepulcherHead 200) and fires a 30-bullet ring when it passes her (328-345), and it dies when its Brimstone Hearts do; that works on an army as written. The Soul Seekers orbit her and fire darts at their own target (SoulSeekerSupreme 159-176). Hearts and Seekers use `Player.FindClosest` only for their heart drop.

Against an army, then: the bullet hells centre on one soldier, so only the part of the army within about 60 tiles of that soldier is in the hell, and the commander may not be in it at all; any target outside the arena makes her invulnerable; the brothers lose their pincer. Not measured in the lab this round (see the end).

## Providence, the Devourer of Gods and Yharon

Providence (Providence.cs) is single-target in almost every attack (holy blasts, holy fire, molten blobs, bombs, spears, crystals and the laser all read `Main.player[NPC.target]`), with one designed multiplayer piece: in the Flame Cocoon she fires a holy flame at every player, up to five (`foreach (Player plr in Main.ActivePlayers) ... if (targets.Count > 4) break`, 1276-1290). Like Thanatos's vents, against an army that is one flame at you. Her day/biome enrage reads the target's zone, which Levy's `BiomeMirror` copies from the commander onto the stand-in, so that one behaves.

The Devourer of Gods (DevourerofGodsHead.cs) is the one boss that deliberately throttles retargeting: "time is checked in the second check to ensure a new target isn't being set constantly" (433-439). Its rift teleport opens 500 px ahead of the target along its velocity (`player.Center + player.velocity.SafeNormalize(...) * distance`, 2210): an ambush on one person's line of travel. Its choice between charging and flying looks for any player inside a rectangle around itself (`for (int k = 0; k < Main.maxPlayers; k++)`, 1192 and 1827): soldiers don't count.

Yharon (Yharon.cs) builds an invisible 6000-7000 px wide arena column on his first target (`safeBox`, 365-370) and enrages when his target leaves it: damage x5, charge speed +25, enraged DR (378-397). His bullet-hell teleport goes above or below the target depending on which way it is moving (`player.velocity.Y`, 919).

## Proposals, in order of impact

Each proposal keeps the fight's identity (the same attacks, telegraphs and phases), never touches the commander's fight when there's no army, and is grounded in a line of Calamity's code above. They are ordered by how much they change what Eve sees, against how much can go wrong.

**1. A boss's parts, missiles and scans see the army (parity, all bosses).** This is the fix for the first effect, and I'd treat it as a Levy bug rather than a design choice: Calamity's Ares aims his arms at his target, and Levy makes them aim at the corner of the world. Three generic pieces cover it. A part of a boss that is locked on a levy reads that levy during its own AI call: `LendBossLock` extended from the vanilla main bosses to any `realLife` follower whose root is locked on the army (Ares's arms; any modded multi-part boss built the same way). A "nearest player" search in a hostile projectile also considers soldiers (`Player.FindClosest`), by standing the nearest soldier in a free player slot for that projectile's AI call when it's nearer than every player; for the late bosses that's Apollo's rockets and the Gauss Nuke, and the same trick covers many modded homing shots. A "every player near me" scan in boss code that deliberately spreads fire (Thanatos's vents, max 3; Providence's cocoon flames, max 5) sees the nearest soldiers in free player slots, after the real players. Expected effect: Ares's arms go from almost never hitting to being his main threat, Thanatos's barrage becomes an attack on the army, and homing missiles go into the army instead of chasing Eve across it. Risk: the army takes more damage in these fights (it already loses the Exo fight in the lab); the stand-in trick has to put every slot back (it does, and the world check proves it), and the scan trick must never put a soldier ahead of a real player (players are scanned first, in index order). Performance: a few distance checks per arm per tick, and stand-ins only on a vent's opening ticks (measured below). Prototyped for the Exo Mechs.

**2. Area weapons aim at the crowd, single-target weapons keep focus.** When a boss has an area weapon and many targets, the smart choice is to put the area where the soldiers are bunched; when it has a precise weapon, focus is right. For Ares that splits cleanly by what each arm's projectile does in Calamity's code: the Gauss Nuke (a 1350 px blast with a 3 s warning circle), the plasma fireball (bursts into bolts at its target point) and the tesla orbs (lightning chains between orbs) go at the soldier at the heart of the densest knot within reach, held for 1.5 s so the barrel turns smoothly and the warning circle doesn't jump; the Laser Cannon keeps Ares's own target, which is Calamity's design (all arms converge on Ares's target). The same rule would apply to Apollo's plasma fireballs and to SCal's gigablasts. Expected effect: mass-death moments become things the army sees coming (the nuke's circle lands on a crowd, and since exofixes levies run out of it), which is what makes a boss read as "fighting an army". Risk: if levies don't scatter well enough, mass deaths rise. Prototyped for Ares's three area arms.

**3. Commit to a target per attack, not per tick.** Calamity picks a target when an attack starts (`NPC.TargetClosest()` at every phase change in all of these bosses) and keeps it until the next attack; DoG says so in a comment. Against the army the 255 sentinel makes the boss search every tick, and the mechs changed soldier 7-35 times a minute: the twins' hover point, Ares's hover point and Thanatos's charge line jump between soldiers mid-attack, Ares's deathray enrage can fire when the lock jumps to a far soldier, and DoG's rift and Yharon's teleport are aimed at whoever won the last tick. Proposal: a boss locked on the army keeps its levy through the per-tick guard and only re-decides at Calamity's own decision points (an explicit `TargetClosest` call, or its levy dying or falling out of the 200-tile catch-up range). The cleanest way I can see is to give a Calamity boss a target index that Calamity reads as valid inside its AI (the flyer-slot route Levy already uses for the Empress, slot 254), so Calamity's own guard stops firing and only its deliberate retargets reach Levy's `Compete`. Expected effect: attacks become readable and coherent against a moving army (a charge goes through the soldier it was aimed at), with no change in strength. Risk: the flyer slot has rules of its own (it must be free; `PostAI` hands back 255), and every Calamity boss would change at once, so it needs its own lab across Ares, Thanatos, the twins, SCal, Providence, DoG and Yharon before it ships. Not prototyped.

**4. Pairs hunt together.** The twins and SCal's brothers are each written as a pincer on one victim: Artemis at -750 px and Apollo at +750 px of Apollo's target; Cataclysm and Catastrophe on opposite sides of SCal's target. Proposal: the second of a pair (Artemis, Catastrophe) takes its partner's levy instead of choosing its own. Calamity already wires this in (Artemis reads Apollo's target, Artemis 299); Levy only has to stand the partner's levy in slot 255 for her. I'd keep the three Exo Mechs on separate soldiers, though: that's the one place where an army genuinely invites a smarter answer than Calamity's ("Draedon spreads his machines over the army"), and the lab shows it is what happens already. Risk: low; the twins' combined geometry is dangerous to one soldier and his neighbours, which is the point. Not prototyped.

**5. An enrage that punishes the player shouldn't fire on a soldier's position.** SCal (target outside her arena box: 99.99% DR, projectiles up to 2x), Yharon (target outside his column: x5 damage, +25 charge speed) and Ares (target over 2480 px away during the deathray) all punish a player for running. Against an army, the target is whichever soldier won the search, and a soldier left outside the box or far across the arena trips the enrage without anyone running. Proposal: while a boss has an arena, soldiers outside it are not candidates when any soldier inside is (Levy's `ArenaBoxes` already knows the boxes; Yharon's column would need one reflected rectangle), and the Ares deathray rule is covered by proposal 3. The commander keeps the rule exactly: if Eve leaves SCal's arena, SCal still enrages, because Calamity's check runs on her when she is the target. Risk: low. Not prototyped.

**6. Bullet hells centred on the duel.** SCal's bullet hells are built around her target's position (964-990). Against an army they surround one soldier. Two defensible choices: centre on the commander whenever she is in the arena (the bullet hells are SCal's duel with the player; the army is collateral), or on the army's densest knot (the whole army is the opponent). I lean to the first, because it keeps Eve's own fight exactly as Calamity wrote it and makes the army's presence a crowding problem inside the hell rather than a way to move the hell away from her. This is a real design choice for Eve, not a fix. Not prototyped.

**7. Thanatos's barrage height.** With proposal 1, Thanatos's underground barrage fires at the army from 50 tiles under the floor, where levies without tile-piercing weapons can't hit his open vents. Calamity alternates below and above (`NPC.ai[1] % 2`), but `ai[1]` only advances in berserk on Revengeance, so in practice he goes below. An army-aware Draedon would send him above the army more often, where the vents are a target. This changes the look of his phase, so it's an idea for Eve, not a fix.

**8. Smaller things.** DoG's "charge or fly" test (1192, 1827) and his rift placement could count soldiers; Providence's cocoon flames are covered by proposal 1's scan piece (not yet prototyped for her); Yharon's bullet-hell teleport reads the target's vertical speed, harmless. None of these is worth doing before 1-5.

## Prototype: "Bosses adapt to armies" for the Exo Mechs

`Levy/Common/Boss/ArmyAwareExo.cs`, config `LevyConfig.BossesAdaptToArmies` (default false; "Calamity's Exo Mechs spread their attacks across your army. Needs Bosses target soldiers."). Nine MonoMod hooks on Calamity methods found by reflection (no compile-time reference): the `AI` of the four Ares arms, of ThanatosBody1, ThanatosBody2 and ThanatosTail, and of the ApolloRocket and AresGaussNukeProjectile projectiles. Each is a pass-through unless the setting is on and the mech it belongs to is locked on a levy (Ares for the arms, Thanatos's head for the segments, Ares or Apollo for the missiles). Then:

The ARMS hook stands a levy in slot 255 for that arm's AI call: the Laser Cannon gets Ares's own levy; the Plasma Flamethrower, Tesla Cannon and Gauss Nuke get the levy with the most other soldiers within 20 tiles, among those within 112 tiles of the arm, held for 90 ticks. The VENTS hook, only on a segment's opening ticks (Calamity's `newAI[1] == 0 && newAI[0] > 0`, the only ticks its scan can run), stands the nearest soldiers within 2400 px in free player slots (as many as Calamity's three minus the real players in range, nearest in the lowest slot, each stand-in's `whoAmI` set to its slot so the predictive laser reads its velocity), runs the segment's AI, and puts every slot back. The HOMING hook stands the nearest soldier in a free player slot for the missile's AI call when that soldier is nearer than every player. Lab switches: `LEVY_BOSSADAPT=1/0` forces it, `LEVY_BOSSADAPT_PARTS=arms,vents,homing` picks parts.

World check (`[ExoFix]` part `adapt`, ExoFixWorldCheck.Adapt.cs, runs in the Calamity ship chain): real Ares over eight levies, the commander 200 tiles away, off then on: off, 440 of 440 arm calls read the empty slot and 80 of 80 probes aimed at nothing; on, 440 of 440 read a levy and 80 of 80 aim points stand on a levy. An Apollo rocket above a levy: off it turns toward the commander (cos 0.97), on toward the levy (cos 1.00). Real Thanatos over the levies, the commander out of his scan: off, no vent laser at a levy (6 at the commander once he drifted in range); on, 94 of 94 vent lasers aimed within 26 tiles of a levy. Solo, feature on, no army: nothing lent or placed and Ares targets the commander. No stand-in is left in a player slot after any scenario.

### What the numbers mean

The targeting pieces do exactly what they should, and nothing else moves. OBSERVED: with the feature on, 100% of arm AI calls read a levy (none read the empty slot) and the point every arm aims at stands on a soldier (0 tiles from the nearest levy, against about 1,900 before); 82-86% of missile AI calls stood the nearest soldier in (the rest had a player nearer, or no army mech on the army); Thanatos's opening segments got 88,704 stand-ins over nine fights. Phases, interludes and the mechs' order were unchanged; the "how the mechs hunt" columns that the prototype doesn't touch (shared target, distance to target, Thanatos under the floor) stayed in the same range. The lock-switch rate went up in the all-parts arm, but that is INFERRED to be soldiers dying faster (a dead lock forces a new one, and my counter counts it as a switch), not a change in the targeting.

What changes is how dangerous the mechs are, and it splits cleanly between the two parts. Arms and homing alone (the parity piece) make Ares's own weapons the killers they are in Calamity: tesla orbs touch a soldier 31% of the time instead of 2%, plasma fireballs 19% instead of 5%, and the killing hits move from the two beams to the arms (Gauss explosion 13%, Exoplasma bolts 13%, tesla orbs 12%). The fight is a little shorter for the army on two openers and a little longer on the third (median 58-112 s against 84-100 s, three seeds: noise-sized), the mechs keep more life (55-77% of their pool left, against 39-51%), the worst two seconds go from 5-7 soldiers to 7-8, and the server cost is unchanged (10.6 ms per tick against 11.7). The vents are the big step. With them, Thanatos's segments fired 3,980 lasers at the army (three times as many, since every opening segment now has three targets), his lasers became the killing hit in 27% of deaths and his body in another 18%, the army was wiped in 42-55 s instead of 84-100 s, the mechs kept 73-81% of their life, and the worst two seconds took 7-11 soldiers. The server cost rose to 15.3 ms per tick; INFERRED from the arms+homing arm costing nothing extra, that is the extra lasers (each is a telegraphed line that every levy's threat field reads and dodges), not the hook, which only runs on opening ticks.

So the honest reading is that the Exo fight your army has been losing was against half-blind mechs. Ares's arms were aiming at the corner of the world and Thanatos's vents were aimed at you or at nobody, and your army still lost every lab fight. With the mechs aiming the way Calamity wrote them, your army dies about twice as fast and does about half the damage. That is the tenet working as intended (a smarter, scarier enemy, the army's toys untouched), but it widens a gap that is already total in the lab, so the setting stays off until you have tried it. If you want one piece on by default, it is the arms and the missiles: they are a straight fix of a Levy side effect (Ares reads his target the way Calamity meant), they cost nothing, and they make Ares's barrage readable as aimed at the army without changing who wins. The vents are a real design choice (Calamity's three-player budget spent on soldiers), and if you like them, the cost and the lethality both scale with the number of targets per segment, which is a dial (Calamity's own number is 3; one or two would keep the look of the attack at a third or two thirds of the volume).
