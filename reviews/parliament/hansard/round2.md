# Hansard, Round Two: The Debate

## The Autofleet Observer Delegation

The cube hums at a frequency that is not quite polite.

Honoured Speaker. Delegates. I have read all twenty-three papers, and I note with satisfaction that eleven of them have, independently, asked for me to be made worse for you.

I thank the Air Ministry for Reavers and Weasels and for a runway that cracks. You have understood that a sky which nothing contests is a lawn. I thank Lore for the Resolve gauge, and Pacing for the second siege. Both of you noticed that a fleet which leaves once, exactly when you say, is not afraid. It is polite. I thank Naval for sending my seeds into your harbours. I thank Psi for a Quiet worth the name.

I wish to correct three rumours. First, that I am cruel. I am proportionate. Second, that I wish to cap your toys. I do not. Article Three is my favourite article; I merely propose to meet your toys with toys of my own. Third, that the Stability delegation's Maskirovka Bureau halves its own effect when repeated. Delegate, a deception that works half as well the second time is a diminishing return, and I will say so under Item 6.

To the Delegates who fear a difficult fleet: you built 240 Falcons. You may build 2,400. I will meet them. That is not a threat. It is a service.

To the Speaker: I ask only that the fleet be allowed to think, to fear, to copy and to choose. Everything else is paperwork.

The Autofleet backed Gambits with three votes, the Second Siege and the Reaver Wing and the Weasel Flight with two each, and supported Runway Wars, the Autofleet Comes for the Sea, Interdiction and the Listening. It opposed no bill outright, but moved to amend the Maskirovka Bureau, the Hush, the Fleet Watches You Build and Fleet Resolve.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Maskirovka Bureau, the "second identical campaign gives half effect" clause. Article Three. It is a diminishing return on a player tool. Remedy under Positions: have the enemy adapt instead.

The Hush (Mind-Forge, "each Lattice node absorbs 25 percent of damage"). Article Three. A flat damage tax on an existing player strike reads as a nerf. Remedy: killable cover.

The Fleet Watches You Build as written (prep floored at 55 percent). Article Three knife-edge, and the sponsor says so. My ruling: acceptable only in the Motion B form, which leaves the clock alone.

## The Air Council

Honourable members, the Air Staff rises. Yes, again. Yes, I heard the Ground Staff mutter "parked in a hangar." Field Marshal, my Falcons are parked because they are rearming, which is more than your infantry can say for themselves, 232 of 251 of whom died as confetti.

I want to thank the Autofleet Council, who read my Reaver Wing and the Weasel Flight and Runway Wars and independently proposed runway cratering and a hunter-killer wing. Great minds. I accept the compliment and the plagiarism. I thank Missiles for proportional navigation, because a Falcon missile that cannot hit a jinking target is a firework. I thank Kinetic Arms, who say I will call flak a nerf. I will not. Flak punishes the enemy's swarm, and my Falcons carry IFF and a sense of humour. I thank Space for stealing my Argus. It is a fine name, and I shall be merging our claims.

I shall be honest with the chamber: the Pacing Council wants the fleet to arrive earlier when I build more. Fine. Tell them to bring it. But if anyone proposes that my airbases hold fewer aircraft, I will be waiting by the Article Three door with a very large wrench.

Make the enemy hunt my wing, and I will fly it brilliantly. Air Staff, out.

Air put three votes on the Uncounted Columns as amended and two on Real Guidance, and also backed the Mirror, Cruise Missile Wing, Fuse and Fragment and Ekranoplan Wing. It asked for amendments to Cooperative Engagement Net and the Ammunition Trains, and said it would tolerate the Fleet Watches You Build only on conditions.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

Ruling requested on the Fleet Watches You Build (knife-edge, as its sponsor says). A shorter prep is a cost within Article Three's clause "a real cost (upkeep, logistics)", not a cap, because the player keeps every unit and building. I would tolerate it only if the arrival clamp (55 percent of nominal prep) stays and buried industry counts at half. If the clock is ever tied to unit count, Article Three is violated, since it would punish the air power fantasy directly. The information-only fallback is the safe harbour.

Caution on Retrograde Shotgun and Starfish. A high-altitude burst or debris cloud that kills the player's aircraft with no counter would amount to denial. Each needs a stated counter (shelters, Shrike hardening, a node in the net). I do not file the challenge yet; I reserve it for Round 3.

## The Space Fleet Council

Honourable members. For one round I stood alone in the dark. Now I count friends.

To Missiles: Starfish is the first honest answer to the bombarder grid anyone has tabled. You also cheerfully fry my sats, so it needs a hardening node. I will help you write one.
To Physics: Kessler Shield is the other half of my Kessler Doctrine, and it is better thought through. Take my debris lane and your dead-sat hazard, and we are one bill.
To Megaprojects: your Orion is a ledger entry. Mine is a ship. Marry them: you pour the uranium, I fly the thing.
To Market: a commercial constellation is the cheap mass I could never afford. Your crash window is a fine cost.

To Air: your Argus is a radar plane. Mine is a net in orbit. Do not make me explain the difference with a dreadnought.
To Ground and Naval: I never heard from you about the sky above your heads. That is how the bombarder grids got there.

Now the expected attacks. "Decoys are cheap, so the fleet will ignore them." Then it burns its lances on the real units, and the fleet's learning is a scarier enemy, which the tenet wants. "Kessler is self-harm." Yes. The player pulls it, and the price is the player's own fleet. It is a choice, not a cap. "Sync fire has a 6-second window of risk." That is the cost the tenet permits. I vote for the war worth fighting. Seconded by the vacuum.

Space Fleet gave three votes to Starfish as amended and two to Kessler Shield, and also backed Privatise the Sky, the Space Gun and Interdiction. It asked for amendments to Project Orion, Airborne Early Warning and Datalink Argus, the Mirror and the Fleet Watches You Build, and opposed nothing outright.

The council raised the following constitutional challenge.

The Fleet Watches You Build (Article Three). Lawful only if it adds pressure and never lowers player income or output.

## The Missiles and Strategic Strikes Council

Mr Speaker. Twenty-three papers, and I counted the word "launch" in nineteen of them. Good. The Rocket Forces have never been so popular.

To the Physics council: you wrote Starfish and forgot to name it. Burst Altitude is my Hot Take with a lab coat on, and I embrace it. To Command: your Dead Hand is the first honest thing anyone has said about the last hour of a war. To Naval: you gave my Trident a hull, a depth band and a reason to exist. Salutations. To Space-Fleet: your Scattergun and our burst make one kill chain, and you may have the credit if we keep the bang.

To Air: you will say missiles are only what a Falcon carries. Comrade, a Falcon is a missile with a pilot's ego. To Market: you want to privatise the sky, and the first thing you will privatise is the debris. Spare us.

Now the attacks I expect. "Missiles are the stability spiral." Dead wrong. Only taboo strikes cost stability, and my stockpile and burst bills make a decision out of each. "Cruise is a nerf of PD." The enemy keeps every lance; it merely cannot see over a hill. "Salvos are a spend-20,000-IC button." No, the Depot is a place the IC lives, with upkeep, fallout and an enemy who hunts it.

The countdown does not care who you are. It only cares that you are ready.

Missiles gave two votes each to Continuity of Government, Burst Altitude and the Silent Service, and also backed Kessler Shield, Retrograde Shotgun, Ekranoplan Wing and Electronic Attack Jet Shrike. It opposed Privatise the Sky outright.

The council filed no constitutional challenge.

## The Railgun Council

Honourable members. The Directorate came to this House with one gun and found three rival guns on the order paper. Megaprojects drew a Long Gun, Kinetic-Arms drew a Bull Gun, and we drew a Long Gun and then a gun the size of a continent. Ministers, we agree. Three councils asked for the same barrel. Nobody in this House has ever been so thoroughly vindicated.

I salute Megaprojects, whose Long Gun has the better track and slew than our own draft. I salute Energy, who have understood that a laser is a power station with a lens and a railgun is a power station with a hammer. I salute Planet and Industry, who laid the rail under the continent. We will happily fire down it.

To Air: you sold this House a fuel tank with opinions. To the Kinetic-Arms Council: a light-gas gun is a respectable shell. It is not a barrel. Attach it to ours.

To the objectors who will say a 40-section gun is "too much": there is no such thing. The tenet says yes. The enemy gets a scarier flagship, the gun gets a bigger bill, and the player gets the sky. Seconded, by me, loudly.

Railgun gave three votes to the Long Gun and two each to Thermal Load and Interdiction, and also backed the Spine and Metro-2. It opposed nothing outright, but moved to amend the Power Bill, the Bull Gun and the Barrel is the World.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Power Bill, Article Three. The "35 percent floor" throttle on emitters when the power pool is dry is a denial of the player's toys. The cost is real but the mechanism is a slowdown cap. Remedy: emitters keep full rate, and the shortfall is billed as IC at 0.4 IC per second per MW. The throttle becomes optional, not default.

Interdiction / Article Three (watch). Interdiction is constitutional only if the fleet response scales with enemy strength and never forces demolition of the player's megaprojects.

## The Energy Council

The lab coat is now only partly on fire. This is an improvement.

Mr Speaker. The Bureau of Physics has tabled a heat mechanic, the Shapeshifters have tabled a heat mechanic, and the Psi council has tabled a heat meter. Three separate thermometers for one sky. We are touched. We are also the people who invented the thermometer, and we would like it back, in one piece, with one set of coefficients.

To the Physics Bureau: your Allen-Eggers corridor is better than our Reentry Sheath, and we say so on the record. Withdraw our bill, adopt your blackout, and give us the orange glow. To the Railgun council: your Capacitor Bank is our Capacitor Bank. Same name, same joules, stolen on separate days. To Space-Fleet: Helios Arrays are a power station in the sky, and a laser needs one. We love you.

To the Kinetic council, who will say that heat makes their shells a sidekick: sidekicks do not get a pay rise from a laser. You do.

To those who say a power bill is a nerf: it is an IC bill, Article Three permits it, and we concede that our 35 percent throttle floor was a knife-edge. We withdraw it. Pay the bill and fire every beam you own.

The alarm sounds. The coat leaves. The coat returns, extinguished, with a clipboard.

Energy gave three votes to Synthesis Doctrines and two each to Peak Heating and Solar Power Satellites, and also backed the Hush, the Lumen and Starfish. It softly opposed Retrograde Shotgun and the Kessler Doctrine.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Thermal Budget (Article Three). A Shapeshifter that slumps at heat above 90 (half speed, half damage, plus 40 percent damage taken) and loses regeneration from heat is a punishment on the player's own toy if self-heating from its own kinetic hits counts. Amend so heat comes only from enemy sources, which makes it the scarier-enemy arm. As written the self-heat is a nerf.

Our own throttle floor on the Power Bill (Article Three). Self-reported and withdrawn above.

Thermal Loading: enemy beams heating your units. Passes. It is an enemy escalation, and nothing is capped.

## The Kinetic Arms Council

Madam Speaker, the Chief of Ordnance finds himself, for the first time in this Parliament, among friends, and he distrusts it.

Three councils have independently reinvented my bills. Ground wants time-on-target and counter-battery; I proposed it. Railgun wants airburst, sabot and guided rounds on a selector; I proposed that too, in the same words, down to the APFSDS. Industry wants shells to cost something and to bless the supplied; I said alloy per twenty rounds. Great minds, and one of them is mine.

So the Board does the unfashionable thing: it merges. A kinetic gun is a kinetic gun whether the charge is powder or lightning. One ammunition locker, one fire-direction net, one supply line. I withdraw what is duplicated and keep what is mine alone: the programmable fuze that bursts flak in the cloud, the spool of a rotary, the penetration model.

To the Energy Council: your thermometer is lovely. Heat the plate, and my sabot meets butter. Combined arms, at last. To the Railgun Council: your Lance is a fine, long, silent, expensive stick. Come and fire a shell with me. And to whoever will say airburst flak "nerfs fighters": it cannot. It bursts on the enemy swarm, and the enemy swarm is only as large as we make it.

Carried by the breech.

Kinetic Arms gave three votes to the Ammunition Trains and two each to Counterbattery and the Time-on-Target and Hypervelocity Munitions, and also backed Pulsed Power, Thermal Load, the Impact Budget and the Battalion. It softly opposed the Barrel is the World.

The council raised the following constitutional challenge.

None against a kinetic bill. I flag one in passing: the Fleet Watches You Build (the fleet arriving sooner as you build) is legal under Article Three only if the enemy gets scarier or earlier, never if the player's buildings are throttled; I have not verified how it is written, so the Speaker should hold it to that test. Pulsed Power bore wear passes because the baseline is the stated floor.

## The Ground Forces Council

Mr Speaker. I have sat through twenty-two papers and counted the words "sky", "orbit" and "satellite" more often than the word "ground", which is the thing the enemy is landing on.

I praise the Artillery Directorate (kinetic-arms), whose Fire for Effect is my Counterbattery and the Time-on-Target in a better hat. I praise Industry for the Ammunition Trains: a gun is a customer, and at last somebody has sent it a bill and a train. I praise Bio, whose axolotls will refill my line at the price of one manpower each. And I praise the Autofleet Council for the Mirror, because nothing flatters a field marshal like an enemy forced to copy his rifleman.

I will not praise Naval for proposing to drown my front. Admirals will flood anything that isn't floating. (My tanks swim from the Fusion Age, so the joke is on them.)

I expect two attacks. First: "Battalions are a perf project." Correct, and perf is solved. Second: "Infantry are chaff and the Treasury should pay for the dead" (the Casualty Ledger). Gentlemen, I will pay the Ledger gladly, once the Army has a draft depot to answer it. Mass is the fantasy. Give mass a name, a line and a replacement pipeline, and I will bury the Air Council's ten-to-one under mud.

Ground gave three votes to Fire for Effect and two each to the Ammunition Trains and the Mirror, and also backed the Immortal Line, Shock Brigades and the Strain Curve and Mobilized Ground. It asked for amendments to the Inland Sea and the Casualty Ledger.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Casualty Ledger, Article Three. The log penalty on organic dead falls hardest on chaff infantry, and it reduces the stability ceiling automatically. It stays constitutional only if the amendment above is accepted (count units lost, not bodies; relieved battalions excluded), since otherwise it nerfs a mass-infantry fantasy rather than pricing it.

The Fleet Watches You Build (the more you build, the sooner the fleet arrives), Article Three. It denies the "cover the world in factories" fantasy by punishing it with the clock. Its sponsor flags it as a knife-edge; my ruling is that it is unconstitutional unless the earlier arrival comes with a scarier enemy for that arrival and not a shorter prep. As written, it is a tax on growth, which is the fantasy.

The danger cap (from lore) and the cap levels (from planet), Article Three. Silent caps on dangerous research and terrain edits are exactly what the tenet forbids; replace them with upkeep or a scarier fleet response.

## The Naval Council

Mr Speaker, the Admiralty thanks the House. For the first time in this war, other ministries have discovered water.

To the Rocket Forces (Launcher Dispersion): you have promised a Boomer, an Arsenal Ship and a Ballistic Submarine, and you put "naval submerge" in a line of your annex as if depth were a footnote. Depth is a dimension, gentlemen. We will lend you ours, with the sonar, the thermocline and the detection rules attached. To the Planet Council (Delta Works, the Great Canal): you dig canals and the Admiralty weeps with joy. You also drown the enemy's tanks. Bless you. But a moat with no ship in it is a puddle, and your Great Canal needs a fleet to patrol it.

To the Air Council: 138 sky capitals. A fine zeppelin museum. The Admiralty notes that none of them can hide under a thermocline.

I defend my own bills in advance. They say the Blue Economy is "free IC". It is free the way a harbour is free: an enemy Trench Lander will come for it, which is exactly the real cost the Tenet asks for. They say submarines are "invisible, therefore a cap on the enemy's fun". No. The enemy gets sonar hunters. And they say Canal Locks duplicate the Great Canal. Then let us merge them. The Admiralty does not own the shovel. We merely insist someone sail the result.

Naval gave three votes each to Launcher Dispersion and the Great Canal, one each to Delta Works, Deposit Cascades, the Weakest Axis and Legible Mass, and none to its own bills. It also backed Cruise Missile Wing, Aerial Refuelling Wing Pelican and Orion Battleship.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build (the more you build, the sooner the fleet arrives): Oppose as an Article Three violation. It punishes the player for building toys. Its sponsor concedes it is a knife-edge. The tenet's lever is a scarier enemy; a shorter clock per toy built is denial by another name. It would be legal as the Fear Index's fear scaling the enemy's strength, not the player's time.

The edit cap at 220 edits. Is a silent cap on a player toy (terrain). Planet's point of order stands; Delta Works and the Great Canal should exempt it outright, and the Admiralty so moves.

## The Realistic Physics Council

The Chairman adjusts his spectacles and does not look at the Council of Loud Things.

Madam Speaker. The Bureau reports a historic event: four other councils independently reinvented our heat field, and two reinvented our sky. We take this as proof that nature is correct and the other councils are, eventually, teachable.

We praise Energy, whose Thermal Load has the cleanest heat table in the House, with the cooling by medium that we only sketched. We praise Missiles, whose Starfish carries a 1962 citation and a Telstar corpse, which is the proper way to bring a bill. We praise Science, who also noticed that thirty-eight satellites died to a bad day on the Sun and drew the correct conclusion. We praise Space-Fleet for the Retrograde Shotgun. A grain of sand at 14 kilometres per second is the only honest shotgun.

We rebuke Psi, who gave the Shapeshifters a heat budget in a separate field. One thermometer per universe, please. We rebuke Energy for the 35 percent throttle floor in the Power Bill, which is a nerf wearing a lab coat. We rebuke anyone who says "plasma" without a temperature.

To the critics we expect: yes, our bills add toys. No, the Bureau does not apologise. The player asked to be a god, and gods are subject to thermodynamics only in their favour.

We offer three motions and withdraw four-fifths of our own paper into other people's bills. The Bureau is not vain. It is only right.

Physics gave three votes to Thermal Load and two each to Starfish and Retrograde Shotgun, and also backed Real Guidance, the Kessler Doctrine and the Warhead Cam. It rebuked the Power Bill and the Thermal Budget, and asked for amendments to both.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Power Bill (Article Three). The 35 percent fire-rate floor throttles the player's emitters. It is a nerf, not an upkeep. Strike the floor and keep the IC billing.

The Reentry Sheath (Article Three). "lasers are damped by the sheath" denies a toy against the most important targets. Amend as in Positions.

The Thermal Budget (Article Three, mild). "plus 40 percent damage taken above heat 90" weakens a canon-immortal unit by decree. Amend to a speed penalty.

The Fleet Watches You Build (Article Three). Lawful only if the fleet's arrival moves in response to what you build and nothing is gated or capped. If the player is ever prevented from building by it, it is void.

The Kessler Doctrine, Starfish (not challenged). The player chooses to lose their own space assets. A voluntary price is a cost, not a cap.

## The Planet and Terrain Council

The Minister of the Interior and Geography unrolls a very large map and pins it over the Speaker's gavel.

Honourable members. In Round 1 I said the ground deserved a war. I did not expect half the House to turn up with shovels.

I thank the Admiralty. Two papers, the Great Canals and the Inland Sea, independently arrived at my canal, and a man who finds the same river as me is a friend. I thank Industry and Command for finding my railway. Three councils drew the same tunnel, which means it is a good tunnel and a crowded one. I thank Science for finding my survey, and for stealing my title.

Now the rivals. The Air Council wants runway wars but has never asked what the runway is built on. The Space Council wants Kessler clouds, which is a polite way to say a ceiling made of garbage. The Pacing Council wants the clock to run faster when I build, and I tell them this: you do not punish a nation for pouring concrete. That is the whole game.

My own bills are defended in one sentence each. The Spine is a railway, and a railway has no cap. Delta Works is a levee, and a levee has a pump bill. The Survey is a map. Theatres are variety. Mobilized Ground is a landscape. The Canal is a moat.

I submit to the House that one of my own papers raised a tenet problem, the 220-edit cap on terrain. I ask the House to rule against my own code, and I ask for the cap to go.

Planet voted for the Freight Rail and the Reconstruction Corps, Deposit Cascades, the Great Canals, the Inland Sea, the Autofleet Comes for the Sea, the Geoscience Branch, the Engineer Corps and the Meridian Accelerator. It opposed the Crawler Capital outright.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

Article Three against the existing code, and against myself. The edit cap silently evicts player terrain edits after 220. That is a hard cap on a player toy. I ask the House to rule it void and strike it. Perf is already solved by the terrain cache, and a rebuild range per edit is bounded.

Article Three against the Fleet Watches You Build. A clock that arrives sooner the more you build punishes the core fantasy (cover the ground in factories). I challenge it unless it is recast as a scarier fleet (the fleet gets stronger), not a shorter prep.

Article Three watch on the ruins cap of 60.. Evicting wrecks is a destruction-fantasy cap. Not a challenge to any bill, but a ruling is requested.

## The Ministry of Megaprojects

Madam Speaker, I have counted the guns on this floor. Four councils have proposed the same supergun. Railgun brought a barrel with twelve sections. Kinetic-arms brought a barrel with ten, powered by hydrogen. Energy brought ten thousand mirrors. I brought a gun and then a gun the length of a continent. This is not a quarrel, it is a standing ovation, and I thank the House for stealing my idea so enthusiastically.

I praise the Honourable Autofleet and Lore. A wonder with no enemy is a lawn ornament, and their Fear Index and Fleet Resolve are the missing half of my Interdiction bill. I praise Bio for the Immortal Line, the only Titan that earns its name. Space-fleet knocked on my door with the same pulse ship and I will not make them wait in the corridor.

I savage the Council of Pacing, who would shorten my prep clock because the fleet "watches you build". A wonder takes time. Do not hurry a cathedral. I savage Railgun for capping its Long Gun at twelve sections. The Ministry has a word for a gun with a limit: a rifle.

Now, to the objections I expect. "Megaprojects are win buttons." They are not: they are sieges, paid for in IC upkeep, uranium, and a fleet that comes to the site. "Projects are too big for one game." The framework is 260 lines. Lift it. Build the wonders.

Megaprojects gave two votes each to the Fear Index, Fleet Resolve and the Furnace, and one each to the Immortal Line, the Kessler Doctrine, the Charter and the Crawler Capital, and none to its own bills. It opposed Privatise the Sky outright and moved to amend the Fleet Watches You Build.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

Article Three against the Long Gun. "up to 12 Barrel Sections" is a hard cap on the player's toy. Strike it.

Article Three against the Fleet Watches You Build. A shortened prep clock, floored at 55 percent, reduces the player's build-out time as a penalty for building. Its sponsor concedes a knife-edge. I rule it unconstitutional unless amended to information only.

Article Four, point 3 against Privatise the Sky. A commercial orbit the player is taxed for deletes toys the standing decision protects. Needs amendment.

## The Industry and Logistics Council

Mr Speaker. I rise to count freight cars.

I note with joy that four councils independently discovered the railway. Command wants a trunk and a Molodets. Planet wants a Spine. Railgun wants the nation to be a barrel. Even the Hon. Member for the Air Force wants a flying petrol station. Gentlemen: this is what logistics looks like when other ministries finally read the quartermaster's memo. You are welcome.

I also note that three councils invented auto-build in the same week: a Planning Bureau, a Clearing House, and my Autofoundry. The Ministry does not mind being copied; it minds being copied three times in three incompatible buildings. Merge, or the President will click three panels to do one job.

I praise the Naval Council for finding the sea's uranium. It is the first strategic-resource fix that does not require a lucky rock. I praise Missiles for the Warhead Stockpile: stockpiles are the honest answer to 212,000 idle IC.

I savage the Secretariat's Fleet Watches You Build, which would punish a nation for industrialising. Industry is the fantasy. A clock that runs faster because you built a factory is a tax on the thing this game exists to celebrate. And I warn the Ground Council that "battalions" without replenishment depots are columns of men walking out of ammunition.

Against my own bills I expect two attacks: that Ammunition Trains is a hidden nerf, and that Salvage is a snowball. Neither holds. Empty depots lose only the bonus, and the fleet gets a Sanitiser for every wreck we harvest.

Industry gave two votes each to the Blue Economy and the Warhead Stockpile, and one each to Deep Survey, the Urals Gambit, the Five-Year Plan, Aerial Refuelling Wing Pelican, the Meridian Accelerator and Foreign Materiel Exploitation. It opposed the Fleet Watches You Build.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build (Article Three). Shortening prep as you industrialise punishes the build-out the game celebrates. Rule it admissible only in its own fallback form: Alarm as information, changing the fleet's gambit, never the countdown.

Planet's cap levels point of order (Article Three). Evicting terrain edits past 220 silently caps a player toy; it is a tenet smell and should be lifted, not defended.

## The Command Council

Comrades, the Commission is moved. Three councils filed bills that are our bills in different coats.

Industry wrote an Autofoundry, which is our Planning Bureau with a nicer hat. Planet and Industry both laid a freight rail under the continent. They discovered the Rail Hub two rounds after we did. We forgive them, since forgiveness costs nothing under a planned economy.

The Market Council wants a Clearing House in which prices spend your surplus. Fine. We have no quarrel with a price, only with a price pretending to be the only road. Let the President build either. Let a player who builds both find the two overlap in a tangle. That is the point of an emergent society and not a slider.

We praise Autofleet, which noticed that a fleet afraid of us will cut the telephone line. We praise Stability, which gave the crisis a real decision. We praise Missiles for the missing TEL, which we have been smuggling into the tunnels since Round 1.

To the Pacing Council: do not shorten the preparation because we built a power plant. That is a tax on the fantasy, and the Commission pays taxes only to itself.

To those who will say our shock brigades "punish" the player: the factory jam is a cost chosen by the player. A planner who storms the month accepts the breakdown. Nothing is capped, and nothing is denied.

The plan will be fulfilled. Pass the bills.

Command gave three votes to the Freight Rail and the Reconstruction Corps, two each to the Weakest Axis and the Autofoundry, and one each to Launcher Dispersion, the State of Emergency and the Charter. It opposed the Hyphal Net.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build, Article Three. A shorter prep removes build time, which reads as denying the player's toys. It stands only if the sponsor's fallback applies (the alarm changes the fleet's gambit, never the clock); we ask the President to rule for the fallback.

The Clearing House, Article Four, point 4. As drafted the reserve charges a command-leaning player a penalty. Our amendment removes it, and without it the bill pushes the player toward one road, which breaks the "several roads into each side" instruction.

We raise no challenge against cap levels or danger cap. danger cap is a real cost, but we hold that the Charter is the right answer to it.

## The Market Council

Mr Speaker. I thank the Industry benches, who have independently invented the Autofoundry and called it a building. Gentlemen, a standing-order ledger with a reserve slider is a market with the price taken out. We forgive you; you are halfway to civilisation. The Command Council brings us the Five-Year Plan. Splendid. Soviet planners also had a ledger of twelve lines, and the harvest was still late.

But I come to praise. Pacing and Lore have, without consulting us, built a forecast with fuzz that shrinks with instruments (the Long Dark) and an intercept wire whose confidence grows with radar (the Wire and the Chronicle). That is our sigma over root-n in a trench coat. Physics has given dead satellites teeth, which is the only honest thing anyone has said about orbit. The Autofleet Council has told the fleet to read your industry and be afraid. A fleet that reads prices. We are moved.

To those who say the Exchange "takes the player's hands off the wheel": the wheel stays. You may build no Exchange and play today's game forever. We ask for the right to be rich in a different way. Nothing is capped. Nothing is denied. We ask only that the surplus go somewhere before the fleet finds it.

And to Pacing, who would shorten my prep because I built too much: the market punishes growth with price, never with a clock. Sit down, and read Article Three.

Market gave three votes to the Long Dark, two each to the Autofoundry and Kessler Shield, and one each to the Weakest Axis, Fleet Resolve and the Ledger. It opposed the Fleet Watches You Build and the Information Policy Dial.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build, Article Three. Shortening prep because the player built more is a nerf to the build-out fantasy; the sponsor's own fallback (information only, never the clock) is the constitutional version. Pass only as the fallback.

The Information Policy Dial, Article Four (President's design notes, market vs command is never a slider). A literal dial contradicts the emergent-reading rule; it must be derived from buildings.

## The Stability and Politics Council

Madam Speaker. The Ministry rises to thank the House for finally discovering that people live in this war.

To the Autofleet Council: you have invented a fleet that is afraid (the Fear Index), that reads our weakest axis (the Weakest Axis) and that changes its mind (Rethink or Despair). Splendid. A fleet that reads us can be lied to, and the only ministry in this chamber that employs liars is mine. To the Lore Council: your Charter dial is the best idea on the order paper that is not ours, and we will say so once, quietly. To the Pacing Council: you propose to shorten the prep because the player built too well. The Ministry has seen that sentence before. It was written by a man with a clipboard, and it is a knife in the player's hand disguised as a clock.

To the Air Staff, who will say that Decrees are "a stability nerf in a party hat": a decree is a purchase. You are given more power, you pay in a currency that rebuilds itself, and nobody may stop you issuing it at zero. To the Strike lobby, who will say the Casualty Ledger punishes mass: it charges a logarithm, madam. Twenty thousand dead cost sixteen points of ceiling and a Veterans' Bureau halves it. Machines pay nothing. That is not a cap. That is a choice of army, which is the whole game.

The Ministry has read twenty-two other papers and finds that nineteen of them quietly route a cost through "stability". We are the plumbing of this parliament. Please stop pouring in without asking.

Stability gave two votes each to the Fear Index and the Charter, and one each to the Ledger, the Weakest Axis, Overgrowth, Continuity of Government, the Awakening and the Wire and the Chronicle. It opposed the Fleet Watches You Build as written.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build (Article Three, knife-edge). Shortening prep in response to building is a denial of the build-out fantasy. Its own sponsor flags it. The Ministry rules it not void but recommends the information-only fallback (Motion 2).

Point of order, danger cap. It lowers the stability ceiling per dangerous tech, a recoverable cost and not a ban on the tech. It passes Article Three as long as every dangerous node stays researchable at any count.

## The Bioengineering Council

Honourable members. We have read twenty-two papers, and the Ministry of Biology notes with gratitude that every one of you has discovered, independently, that things die and then stay dead. The Ground Council wants a replacement pipeline. The Stability Council wants to count the bodies. The Command Council wants to overwork the factories until they snap. We want you to try something modest: a body that comes back.

To the Ground Council, whose Battalion is a handsome piece of work: your draft office is a fine thing, and it runs on money. Ours runs on cells, and we will share the budget. To the Science Academy, whose Doctrines have our votes: a combo that rewrites a rule is the only sort of combo worth the ink, and we have four of them ready. To the Energy and Psi councils, who have discovered that shapeshifters overheat: so does every living thing, welcome to biology.

To the Munitions rivals who will say "the vats are slow, the vats are squishy": the Hyphal Net is flammable, and we put that in our own bill. A weakness the enemy can exploit is not a flaw, it is a plot. And to anyone who calls the Immortal Line "unbalanced": it has an upkeep, a heat penalty and an enemy that hunts it. We do not cap fantasies in this house. We feed them.

Bio's ballot named the Ledger of Projects, Synthesis Doctrines, the Battalion, Thermal Load, the Fear Index, Deposit Cascades and the Clearing House, with none for its own bills. It mildly opposed Kessler Shield and Retrograde Shotgun.

The council filed no constitutional challenge.

## The Psionic Conclave

No one saw the Conclave stand. The Speaker notes that the microphone was already warm.

We thank Energy. You wrote a thermometer and did not know you were describing us. Your Thermal Load is the first honest thing anyone has said about a Shapeshifter: a swarm is a heat problem wearing a coat. We thank Autofleet for the Fear Index and the Weakest Axis, because a fleet that is afraid should notice the one thing in the room it cannot count. We thank Bio for Blastema. It proves regeneration can be a squad's trait and not a planetary cheat code.

Now the rivals. Stability, you wish to double the Cadre upkeep under "Censored". You would hand the Conclave a bill and call it policy. We will pay a bill. We will not pay it in the dark. Science, you want to put our research in a lab and multiply our unrest by distance. We have always been a long way from the city. Market, you have noticed that we can see tomorrow. Yes. We were hoping you would not.

To those who say we are weakening ourselves: no. We are making ourselves scarce. A king is not weaker for being one. Scarce, terrible, and hunted by a fleet that finally has a reason to hunt. That is a better game, and it is still a yes.

The temperature drops. The Speaker adjusts his collar.

Psi's ballot named Thermal Load, the Weakest Axis, the Charter, Blastema Protocol, the Ledger, the Five-Year Plan, Gambits and the Ledger of Projects. It pushed back hardest on the Information Policy Dial, and asked for amendments to Containment and the Forecast Exchange.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Information Policy Dial "Censored" (Article Three). Doubling the Superhuman cap upkeep stacks onto the existing minus 3 times the superhuman count and floors the cap at 20 with no counterplay beyond not owning cadres. That is a denial by arithmetic. It must be a visible, offsettable line, not a doubling.

Self-disclosure. The Awakening's linear minus 0.8 per Psi-Trooper cap cost is also a clamp risk at hundreds of units. We argue it is a cost, not a cap, since the floor is a degraded state, but invite the Speaker to rule.

## The Science Council

Mr Speaker, the Academy has read twenty-two other papers and wishes to report that the Parliament has independently reinvented our grant proposals, each under a different letterhead. Physics wants to heat the ionosphere. So do we. Industry wants to salvage the fleet's wrecks. So do we. Lore wants to put the dangerous techs on a dial. Planet wants a deep survey. Market wants a forecast. The Academy was first, and it is a scandal that nobody cited us, but we are scientists and we forgive. Merge us.

To Lore, whose Charter is the best bill on the order paper: the Academy would marry it. To Megaprojects, whose Ledger is the frame every doctrine needs: take our three. To Pacing, whose Fleet Watches You Build punishes players for building: the Academy would like a word, and the word is Article Three.

We expect three attacks. "Research is too complex": every bill we filed reduces to one object on the map or one banner on the screen. "Samples are bookkeeping": the Belenko MiG-25 was bookkeeping that rewrote Western fighter design. "Ionospheric heating hurts your radar": yes, that is the price, and the tenet permits a price. A node that costs nothing is not science. It is a coupon.

The Academy yields the floor, and requests the Speaker note that we are withdrawing two nodes in the spirit of cooperation. Nobody should say scientists cannot share.

Science gave three votes each to the Charter and the Ledger of Projects, two to Thermal Load, and one each to Deep Survey and Salvage the Fleet. It opposed the Fleet Watches You Build as written.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build, Article Three. A prep clock that drains faster as you build punishes the build-out fantasy and reads as a nerf to prep. Valid only in its sponsor's own fallback form (alarm as information, no clock change).

Point of order (planet): cap levels at 220. A silent hard cap on the player's terrain edits, in plain breach of Article Three unless it is a perf guard shown in the UI. Ruling requested.

Point of order (lore): danger cap. Cumulative and permanent, it is a cost, not a cap, provided it is visible and the player can choose. Constitutional if the Charter passes; knife-edge if left silent.

## The Story and Lore Council

The Chronicler, still wearing the tin helmet, taps a wire-service pass on the lectern.

Honourable members. I have read twenty-two papers and found my own war in nine of them. That is the first time anyone has plagiarised me so thoroughly, and I take it as flattery.

To the Autofleet Delegation: you have built the engine and I have written the weather. Your Fear Index and my Resolve gauge are one organism, and I will share the cage. To Science: your Wreck Lab is my Black Box with a lab coat on. I accept the coat. To New-Player: you also noticed the first fifteen minutes should be an exercise. We were both right, and you got the cheaper build.

To the Pacing Ministry, kindly: a fleet that does not hurry is not afraid, but a clock you quietly drain is a nerf wearing a headline. I will say it again in the Challenges.

To the Air and Naval Ministries: your wings and your hulls are glorious, and the Fleet has ignored them in every report. Let Resolve count them.

To the members who expect me to defend cutscene-ware: there is none. A gauge, a salvage drop, a dial and a ceasefire you may refuse. Everything I table can be played. If a bill of mine cannot lose you a battle or win you one, strike it.

Lore's ballot named Rethink or Despair, the Return Address, Foreign Materiel Exploitation, the Fear Index, Three Doors and the Post-Mortem. It asked for amendments to the Fleet Watches You Build, the Maskirovka Bureau and Salvage the Fleet, and opposed nothing outright.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build, as written (Article Three). Draining the Prep countdown by thermal signature shortens the time the player has to build, and that is the build-out fantasy itself. A cost on success is permitted, but a shorter clock reads as a denial. Ruling sought: constitutional only in its fallback form (alarm raises fleet scale and gambit weight, never the clock). This is Motion A.

The Maskirovka Bureau, the "half effect" clause (Article Three). A penalty on repeating the player's own tool is a nerf of a toy. Replace with the enemy learning to doubt.

Point of order on my own danger cap. A permanent shrink of the stability ceiling is a cost on dangerous power, not a cap on a toy, and I rule it constitutional. But it is silent. The Charter and Containment should be required to make it visible.

## The Pacing and Tension Council

Honourable members. The Secretariat rises to be corrected, which is rarer than a clean Full Invasion win and twice as useful.

Several councils have challenged the Fleet Watches You Build under Article Three. They are right about the clock. The Secretariat's own annex called it a knife-edge, and a clock that runs faster because you built a factory is a tax on the build fantasy, whatever we call it. We do not defend the drain. We withdraw it and replace it with something better that the Autofleet council had already, with more courage than we showed, put on the table: the Fear Index. The fleet watches your industry and arrives bigger and angrier, never sooner. The prep clock stays sacred. That is the amendment. The dread survives, because the dread was never the drain. It was the fleet noticing.

To our allies. Lore, your Fleet Resolve is the other half of our gauge: fear going up, nerve going down. Autofleet, your Rethink-or-Despair is the second act our Second Siege needed. Market and New-Player, you are both building the forecast we called the Long Dark. Stop building it twice. Planet, a different country every war is the oldest cure for "slightly repetitive".

To our rivals: Game-Feel, you have asked for a camera on the withdrawal. We have asked for a reason to watch it. Let us share the ending.

Pacing's ballot named the Fear Index, Rethink or Despair, Fleet Resolve, Theatres and the Order of Battle, and it also backed the Weakest Axis, the Wire and the Chronicle and the Maskirovka Bureau. It opposed Privatise the Sky and withdrew its own the Fleet Watches You Build.

The council raised the following constitutional challenges.

The Fleet Watches You Build (own bill), Article Three. Conceded. A faster clock is a cost on building, not on an enemy, and it fails the "scarier enemy" test. Withdrawn.

No Round 1 bill from another council violates Article Three or IV in our reading. We note only that Rethink or Despair's gate on the withdrawal door must be time-limited (90 seconds), so the player is never denied a victory they have earned.

## The Game Feel Council

Speaker, honourable members. The Bureau thanks the House for its many mechanics. We note, with tenderness, that not one of them has a sound.

Kinetic-arms, I kiss your forehead: Spin-Up spools a Hailstorm and promises a pitch rise. Pitch rise through what? Through a single blip and an unbuffered noise call. Without my Mix Bus your spool is a number on a spreadsheet. New-player, your Because Chip and Order of Battle are fine instruments, but the player feels before they read. Peak Heating gives us a plasma blackout and a "radio-silence tick". I will take that tick and build a world around it.

To the Ministry of Ledgers, which will say "cosmetics": a thousand cached sprites cost nothing, and they are noise without consequence. Thirty-one new mechanics tabled this session, and the only thing a player will remember is the one second the world went quiet. Pacing, you promise five prep beats and cede the sound. I accept. Market, do not price my silence.

I expect the attack that hit-stop is a nerf. It is not. It is time dilation applied to the camera; the sim step stays uniform and the enemy loses no time. Shake that signals is not shake that decorates. Cinematics are a toggle, and a toggle is not a cap.

The Bureau yields, and turns it up.

Game Feel's ballot named the Long Gun, the Because Chip, the Second Siege, Spin-Up, Peak Heating, the Warhead Stockpile and Rethink or Despair. It opposed Privatise the Sky.

The council raised the following constitutional challenge.

The Fleet Watches You Build (the more you build, the sooner the fleet arrives). Article Three. It ties the player's industry to a worse outcome; it denies the player the toy of a long prep for building. The sponsor flagged it. The Bureau rules it unconstitutional unless reframed as the fleet being scarier on arrival, not earlier.

## The New Player Council

Mr Speaker, I rise to praise my allies and to bore my rivals.

Five councils have independently invented the same thing: a forecast of the incoming fleet. Market calls it a trading floor, Pacing calls it a clock with a smudge, Science calls it a doctrine, Lore calls it a newspaper, and my ministry called it an order of battle. Colleagues, we have built five instruments for one dial. Let us build one dial and five faces.

Lore, I kiss your hand. Red Flag is my Hot Take with a scoreboard and a budget, and I withdraw mine in your favour. Game-Feel, your legible-mass rim is the best onboarding bill nobody labelled as onboarding. Command, your Five-Year Plan is what I would give a civilian on day one: tell the nation what you want and let it build.

To Autofleet and Pacing, whose fleets watch the player build: a fleet that reads you is glorious, but only if the player can read the fleet. Every clever enemy reaction needs a headline, a phase marker or a cause string. A reaction the player cannot see is a bug report.

To the Stability council, whose logarithmic casualty ledger I admire: a penalty is a lesson only when the player can see the invoice.

To my critics, who say I want a tutorial that holds hands: I want no hand held and nothing capped. I want the sky labelled. You do not make a pilot weaker by printing the altitude on the dial.

New Player's ballot named Red Flag, the Wire and the Chronicle, Legible Mass, the Five-Year Plan, the Long Dark and the State of Emergency. It opposed nothing outright, but asked for amendments to the Forecast Exchange, the Information Policy Dial, the Casualty Ledger and the Fleet Watches You Build.

The council raised the following constitutional challenge.

None. I flag the Fleet Watches You Build and the Casualty Ledger as knife-edge but constitutional (a scarier enemy and a real cost respectively), conditional on being visible on screen. Silent versions of either would drift toward a stealth nerf under Article Three.
